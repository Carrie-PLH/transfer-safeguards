Kansas

Grounds stated in the sources
Not stated in the sources reviewed. K.S.A. 39-936(g), the only captured Kansas provision governing an involuntary move, states a notice requirement and no grounds at all: it bars an involuntary transfer or discharge "unless the resident or legal guardian of the resident has been notified in writing at least 30 days in advance of a transfer or discharge of the resident." — "Except in emergencies as defined by rules and regulations of the licensing agency and except as otherwise authorized under federal law". The regulations that once stated the grounds, K.A.R. 28-39-147 and 28-39-148, appear in the Secretary of State's regulation volume as "28-39-147 and 28-39-148. (Authorized" — "Nov. 1, 1993; amended Feb. 21, 1997; revoked" — with a revocation date of May 22, 2009, and no replacement for nursing facilities was found in that volume. What the state's ombudsman office states, describing the federal law rather than a Kansas rule, is that a resident may "Remain in the nursing facility unless a transfer or discharge:" is necessary for the resident's welfare, is appropriate because the resident's health has improved, "is needed to protect the health and safety of other residents or staff;" or follows a failure to pay after reasonable notice.
Notice stated in the sources
Thirty days, from the statute: written notice to the resident or legal guardian "at least 30 days in advance", with an exception for emergencies as the licensing agency's regulations define them and for what federal law otherwise authorises. The statute states nothing about what the notice must contain. The ombudsman office states the federal content requirement — a resident should "Receive thirty-day notice of transfer or discharge which includes the reason, effective date, location to which the resident is transferred or discharged, the right to appeal, and the name, address, and telephone number of the state long-term care ombudsman".
Hearing office in these sources
Not stated in the sources reviewed. No captured Kansas source names an office, an address, a form or a deadline for appealing an involuntary transfer or discharge. The statute states the notice period and stops; the regulations that carried the transfer and discharge rights were revoked in 2009; the ombudsman office refers to "the right to appeal" as part of what the federal notice must state, and names no Kansas forum.
Ombudsman in these sources
The Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, an office of its own rather than a programme inside a service agency. Its published contact: "Kansas Long-Term Care Ombudsman" at "900 SW Jackson, Suite 1041 Topeka, Kansas 66612", "Toll FREE: 1 (877) 662 - 8362". On this subject the office states: "Involuntary Discharge continues to be one of the top complaints that ombudsmen report here in Kansas and nationally."

LedeWhat this page holds

Lede

This page assembles what Kansas's own publishers state about involuntary transfer and discharge from an adult care home, and it is a thin page because the state's own materials are thin. The fullest single source is one subsection of one statute, K.S.A. 39-936(g), which requires thirty days' written notice and says nothing else: no grounds, no contents, no appeal, no forum. Kansas's vocabulary is transferred from or discharged from an adult care home involuntarily.

The reason the page is short is worth stating plainly. Kansas once had regulations on this subject — K.A.R. 28-39-147 and 28-39-148 — and the Secretary of State's own regulation volume records them as revoked on May 22, 2009, printing the pair as a bare citation with no text under it. Nothing in that volume's nursing facility article replaces them. So what a Kansas resident has, in Kansas's own published law, is a thirty-day notice and an emergency exception defined by regulations that no longer exist for this purpose. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on the federal page; nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

01The grounds, as Kansas describes them

The grounds, as Kansas describes them

Not stated in the sources reviewed. K.S.A. 39-936(g) — Kansas Office of Revisor of Statutes, history line ending L. 2024, ch. 103, § 3; July 1, retrieved 2026-08-30 — is the only Kansas statute located governing an involuntary transfer or discharge, and it states a notice period and no grounds: "Except in emergencies as defined by rules and regulations of the licensing agency and except as otherwise authorized under federal law, no resident may be transferred from or discharged from an adult care home involuntarily unless the resident or legal guardian of the resident has been notified in writing at least 30 days in advance of a transfer or discharge of the resident."

The regulations that once enumerated grounds, K.A.R. 28-39-147 and 28-39-148, appear in the Secretary of State's 2022 regulation volume — Kansas Secretary of State (PDF), 2022 volumes, retrieved 2026-08-30 — as a bare citation with no text beneath it, in a short run of consecutive revocations: "28-39-147 and 28-39-148. (Authorized" "by and implementing K.S.A. 39-932; effective" "Nov. 1, 1993; amended" "May 22, 2009.)" — a revocation date, read together with the adjoining entries for 28-39-145a and 28-39-146 (both also "revoked May 22, 2009" in the same volume), matching the pattern CLAUDE.md records for this exact PDF: a section number followed by a bare authorization-and-revocation line and nothing else. No replacement provision stating grounds for nursing facilities was found in that volume's article 39.

The state's own long-term care ombudsman office, describing federal rather than Kansas law, states that under "The Nursing Home Reform Law of 1987", a resident's right is to "Remain in the nursing facility unless a transfer or discharge:" "is necessary to meet the resident's welfare;" "is appropriate because the resident's health has improved and s/he no longer requires nursing home care;" "is needed to protect the health and safety of other residents or staff;" "is required because the resident has failed, after reasonable notice, to pay the facility charge for an item or service provided at the resident's request". This is quoted as the ombudsman office's own description of the federal Nursing Home Reform Law, not as a statement of Kansas law; the federal enumeration itself is assembled on the federal page and is not restated here as Kansas's.

02The published process

The published process

Requesting the hearing

Not stated in the sources reviewed for a nursing facility's own transfer or discharge decision specifically. No captured Kansas statute or regulation names an office, address, form, or deadline for appealing such a decision; K.S.A. 39-936(g) states only the notice period, and the regulations that once carried an appeals process (28-39-147/148) are revoked with no replacement located.

One first-party Kansas forum was located this pass, and its connection to a nursing facility's own transfer or discharge decision is not stated anywhere in the sources reviewed. KanCare, the state's Medicaid program, publishes a "State Fair Hearings" process for two named kinds of dispute — a Medicaid eligibility decision, and a healthcare service decision made by one of the program's managed care organizations — neither of which the page names as covering a nursing facility's own decision to transfer or discharge a resident. For a managed-care service decision, the page states: "The KanCare state fair hearing is processed by the Office of Administrative Hearings (OAH). It is an opportunity for the member to speak about the healthcare service decision made by one of the Medicaid agency's managed care organizations (MCO)". The filing route: "The member or member's authorized representative's state fair hearing request must be submitted in writing" by fax "(785) 296-4848" or by mail to "Office of Administrative Hearings" "1020 S. Kansas Ave." "Topeka, KS 66612". The deadline: "the member or member's representative must submit a request for a state fair hearing within 123 calendar days from the date of the notice of the appeal resolution determination." Whether an involuntary nursing-facility transfer or discharge is a "healthcare service decision made by" an MCO in the sense this page uses the phrase is not stated in the sources reviewed, and this page does not assert that it is.

The notice's required contents

Not stated in the sources reviewed, beyond what the ombudsman office describes as federal law. K.S.A. 39-936(g) states the thirty-day period and nothing about what the notice must contain. The ombudsman office states that under federal law a resident should "Receive thirty-day notice of transfer or discharge which includes the reason, effective date, location to which the resident is transferred or discharged, the right to appeal, and the name, address, and telephone number of the state long-term care ombudsman" — again, quoted as that office's description of federal requirements rather than a Kansas statute or regulation's own text, since none stating notice contents was located.

Bed-hold and return

Not stated in the sources reviewed. Neither K.S.A. 39-936 nor the Secretary of State's 2022 regulation volume for article 28-39 states a bed-hold or readmission provision; the regulations that might once have carried one are among those revoked in 2009. A Kansas Medicaid nursing-facility payment manual, referenced by title in general search results as covering reserved-bed and leave-day payment, could not be captured this pass: the host serving it, portal.kmap-state-ks.us, sits behind an Oracle Access Manager single-sign-on redirect, and every transport tried — curl following the redirect chain, the session's web-fetch tool, and the session's built-in browser navigating directly to the document — returned a session-bound token or a file-download prompt rather than the document's own text. Nothing from that manual is quoted anywhere on this page; it is capture-pending.

Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

Thirty days' advance notice — K.S.A. 39-936(g): "no resident may be transferred from or discharged from an adult care home involuntarily unless the resident or legal guardian of the resident has been notified in writing at least 30 days in advance of a transfer or discharge of the resident", with an exception "in emergencies as defined by rules and regulations of the licensing agency and except as otherwise authorized under federal law".

A hearing deadline of a different kind, from a forum of uncertain applicability — KanCare's State Fair Hearings page, on a managed-care service decision: "the member or member's representative must submit a request for a state fair hearing within 123 calendar days from the date of the notice of the appeal resolution determination." Whether this period applies to a nursing facility's own transfer or discharge decision is not stated in the sources reviewed.

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the relevant state process to determine.

Delaware

Grounds stated in the sources
16 Del. Admin. Code § 3102-3.1: "The facility must permit each resident to remain in the facility, and not transfer or discharge the resident from the facility unless—" "The transfer or discharge is necessary for the resident's welfare and the resident's needs cannot be met in the facility."; "The transfer or discharge is appropriate because the resident's health has improved sufficiently so the resident no longer needs the services provided by the facility;"; "The safety of individuals in the facility is endangered;"; "The health of individuals in the facility would otherwise be endangered;"; "The resident has failed, after reasonable and appropriate notice, to pay for (or to have paid under Medicare or Medicaid) a stay at the facility."; "The facility ceases to operate." A second list in the same current regulation, § 3102-4.2, for "Nursing Facility and Similar Facility" residents, states five grounds differently: "Medical needs which cannot be met in the facility;"; "The resident's own welfare;"; "The welfare of the other individuals in the facility;"; "Nonpayment of justified charges, after appropriate notice;"; "Termination of facility operation." Both lists are quoted as published; neither is reconciled here.
Notice stated in the sources
Before a transfer or discharge, the facility must "Notify the resident and, if known, a family member or legal representative of the resident of the transfer or discharge and the reasons for the move in writing and in a language and manner they understand." The notice is due "at least 30 days before the resident is transferred or discharged" except that "Notice may be made as soon as practicable before transfer or discharge when:" "The safety of individuals in the facility would be endangered under paragraph 3.1.3 of this section;"; "The health of individuals in the facility would be endangered, under paragraph 3.1.4 of this section;"; "The resident's health improves sufficiently to allow a more immediate transfer or discharge, under paragraph 3.1.2 of this section;"; or "An immediate transfer or discharge is required by the resident's urgent medical needs under paragraph 3.1.1 of this section." The required contents include "A statement of the resident’s right to a fair hearing as provided in this section;", "The method by which the resident may request a fair hearing;", and "The name, address and telephone number of the State long term care ombudsman;" (§ 3102-3.5).
Hearing office in these sources
"An impartial hearing may be requested by a resident who believes a facility has erroneously determined that he or she must be transferred or discharged." The hearing request must "Be in writing;", "Be received by the facility within 30 days from the date that the discharge notice is received by the resident or the resident’s legal representative;", and "Be copied to the Division and the State LTC ombudsman." (§ 3102-5.1.1) — the regulation names no separate filing address, form, or administrative-hearings office beyond the facility itself and those two copies. On pendency, the same regulation states: "If the resident appeals a notice of discharge based on this section, the facility will not be cited during the pendency of the appeal for housing a resident whose needs exceed the permitted level of care in that facility." (§ 3102-3.1.1). The regulation's own definitions name "the Division" as "the Division of Long Term Care Residents Protection." (§ 3102-2.0), a name that does not appear on the department's current site, which instead directs nursing-facility complaints and correspondence to the Division of Health Care Quality's Office of Long-Term Care: "263 Chapman Road, Suite 200" "Newark, DE 19702-5408" "Phone: (302) 421-7400" "Toll-Free Hotline: 1-877-453-0012". Both names are quoted as published and not reconciled.
Ombudsman in these sources
The regulation requires every transfer or discharge notice to carry "The name, address and telephone number of the State long term care ombudsman;" (§ 3102-3.5.7). The program's own page: "The Delaware Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program advocates for and protects those who live in long-term care settings, such as skilled nursing and assisted living facilities, as well as those who receive home and community-based services." Its published contact: "Contact the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman:" "DHSS_OSEC_Ombudsman@Delaware.gov" "1-855-773-1002". Elsewhere on the same page the address is printed in a different case: "email" "DHSS_OSEC_Ombudsman@delaware.gov" — both quoted as published, not reconciled.

LedeWhat this page holds

Lede

This page assembles what Delaware's own publishers state about involuntary transfer and discharge from a nursing facility. The fullest single source is 16 Del. Admin. Code § 3102, Long Term Care Transfer, Discharge and Readmission Procedures, last amended per its own closing citation "16 DE Reg. 296 (09/01/12)", which states the grounds (twice, in two parallel tracks for two different categories of facility), the thirty-day notice and its exceptions, the notice's required contents, and the fair-hearing procedure in a single regulation. Delaware's own vocabulary is transfer or discharge, and the appeal is called a fair hearing.

The regulation is the sole publisher of the hearing procedure — it names no separate administrative-hearings office, filing address, or form; a resident's hearing request goes to the facility itself, copied to "the Division" and the state ombudsman. The regulation's own definition of "the Division" no longer matches the name the department's current site uses for nursing-facility oversight, a discrepancy this page records rather than resolves. The state Medicaid bed-hold duration the regulation points to by name and section number is a fourth publisher, the DHSS Long Term Care Institutional Provider Specific Policy Manual. The state long-term care ombudsman program is a separate publisher, the Office of the Secretary's own page, for its contact details. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on the federal page; nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

01The grounds, as Delaware describes them

The grounds, as Delaware describes them

16 Del. Admin. Code § 3102-3.1 (Delaware Administrative Code, regulations.delaware.gov, PDF, 16 DE Reg. 296 (09/01/12), retrieved 2026-09-26), for residents of "a certified skilled nursing facility or a certified nursing facility as defined in 42 CFR §483.5 or an Intermediate Care facility (ICF/MR) as defined in 42 CFR §440.150", states: "The facility must permit each resident to remain in the facility, and not transfer or discharge the resident from the facility unless—", in the regulation's own order:

"The transfer or discharge is necessary for the resident's welfare and the resident's needs cannot be met in the facility."

"The transfer or discharge is appropriate because the resident's health has improved sufficiently so the resident no longer needs the services provided by the facility;"

"The safety of individuals in the facility is endangered;"

"The health of individuals in the facility would otherwise be endangered;"

"The resident has failed, after reasonable and appropriate notice, to pay for (or to have paid under Medicare or Medicaid) a stay at the facility."

"The facility ceases to operate."

A second list in the same current regulation, § 3102-4.2, states the grounds differently for residents of "a Nursing Facility and Similar Facility as defined in 16 Del.C. 1102(4)": the facility "must permit each resident to remain in the facility, and not transfer or discharge the resident from the facility except for:"

"Medical needs which cannot be met in the facility;"

"The resident's own welfare;"

"The welfare of the other individuals in the facility;"

"Nonpayment of justified charges, after appropriate notice;"

"Termination of facility operation."

Both lists are current and both are quoted as published; the regulation does not say which applies to a given facility beyond the two definitions above, and this page does not reconcile them.

02The published process

The published process

Requesting the hearing

§ 3102-5.1.1: "An impartial hearing may be requested by a resident who believes a facility has erroneously determined that he or she must be transferred or discharged." The hearing request must "Be in writing;", "Be received by the facility within 30 days from the date that the discharge notice is received by the resident or the resident’s legal representative;", and "Be copied to the Division and the State LTC ombudsman." The regulation names no separate filing address, form, or administrative-hearings office beyond the facility itself and those two copies. On pendency, § 3102-3.1.1 states, of a transfer or discharge based on the resident's-welfare ground: "If the resident appeals a notice of discharge based on this section, the facility will not be cited during the pendency of the appeal for housing a resident whose needs exceed the permitted level of care in that facility." The regulation states no equivalent pendency sentence for the other five grounds at § 3.1 or for the § 4.2 track. § 5.2 states two circumstances in which "DHSS may deny or dismiss a request for a hearing": "The resident withdraws the request in writing; or" "The resident or his or her legal representative fails to appear at a scheduled hearing without good cause." § 5.10.2 states that the impartial fact-finder's decision is "the final decision of DHSS with the right to an appeal pursuant to the Administrative Procedures Act, Title 29, Chapter 101."

The notice's required contents

§ 3102-3.5, for the § 3.0 track, states the written notice "must include the following:" "A detailed individualized explanation of the reason(s) for the action being taken which includes, in terms understandable to the resident:" a statement of the intended action, the reasons for it including, "When the reason is non-payment, an itemized statement of the resident’s account for the preceding 12 months", and "The specific policy or regulation supporting such action."; "The effective date of transfer or discharge;"; "The location to which the resident will be transferred or discharged;"; "A statement of the resident’s right to a fair hearing as provided in this section;"; "The method by which the resident may request a fair hearing;"; "A statement that the resident may represent him or herself or may be represented by counsel or by another person."; and "The name, address and telephone number of the State long term care ombudsman;". § 3102-4.6, for the § 4.0 track, states substantially the same list with one addition: "For nursing facility residents with a developmental disability or mental illness, the mailing address and telephone number of the Delaware protection and advocacy agency as defined in Title 16 Del.C. §1102." Both tracks' § 3.3 and § 4.4 require the facility to "Notify the resident and, if known, a family member or legal representative of the resident of the transfer or discharge and the reasons for the move in writing and in a language and manner they understand." A copy of that notice must also go, per § 3.3.2: "Provide a copy of the notice to the Division; the State LTC ombudsman; the resident’s Delaware Medicaid managed care organization (MCO), if any; any DHSS agency involved in the resident’s placement in the facility, including APS; and the protection and advocacy agency as defined in Title 16 Del.C. §1102 if the resident is an individual with a developmental disability or mental illness."

Bed-hold and return

§ 3102-3.9.1: before a transfer to a hospital or therapeutic leave, the nursing facility must give written information specifying "Notice of State bed-hold. The duration of the bed-hold policy under the State plan, if any during which the resident is permitted to return and resume residence in the nursing facility, See DHSS Long Term Care Institutional Provider Specific Policy Manual at Section 4.5" and "Facility policies. The nursing facility's policies regarding bed-hold periods". That manual's own Section 4.5 (Long Term Care Institutional Provider Specific Policy Manual, Delaware Division of Medicaid & Medical Assistance, PDF, Revision Table: 02/01/2023 manual-wide, Section 4.5 last revised 9/1/2016, retrieved 2026-09-26) states: "If a member is hospitalized for a short period of time and is expected to return to the LTC facility, payment may continue for a period of not more than 7 days provided that the LTC facility agrees to hold the bed for the resident." and "DMAP reimbursement is available for only 7 days within each 30-day period." A separate rate applies to an Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities: "payment may continue for a period of not more than 14 days provided that the ICF/IID agrees to hold the bed for the resident." with DMAP reimbursement likewise "available for only 14 days within each 30-day period for members residing in an ICF/IID." On return, § 3102-3.9.3 requires the facility to "establish and follow a written policy under which a resident, whose hospitalization or therapeutic leave exceeds the bed-hold period under the State plan, is readmitted to the facility immediately upon the first availability of a bed in a semi-private room" if the resident "Requires the services provided by the facility" and "Is eligible for Medicaid nursing facility services." The § 4.0 track states a different return right at § 3102-4.10.1 and 4.10.2: the facility must provide written information specifying "that the facility must accept the patient or resident back into the facility when the resident no longer needs acute care and there is space available in the facility. If no space is available, the resident shall be accepted into the next available bed." and the facility must "establish and follow a written policy for implementing its obligation to immediately offer the first available bed to a resident who is entitled to be readmitted to the facility when acute care is no longer required." Neither track's own bed-hold text states a day count for the § 4.0 track separately from the Section 4.5 manual quoted above.

The three rows above hoist the questions a facility's counsel answers in the first fifteen minutes. They are quotations from Delaware's published sources, not a determination that any of them applies to any situation.

Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

30 days' advance notice — § 3102-3.4.1 and, in the § 4.0 track's own wording, § 3102-4.5: both state that the notice "must be made by the facility at least 30 days before the resident is transferred or discharged." Notice "may be made as soon as practicable before transfer or discharge" under § 3.4.2 when safety or health of others in the facility would be endangered, the resident's health has improved sufficiently to allow a more immediate move, or an immediate move is required by urgent medical needs; § 4.5.1 states the equivalent as-soon-as-practicable circumstances for the § 4.0 track, adding "or after transfer or discharge" to that track's own wording.

30 days to request the hearing — § 3102-5.1.1.2: the hearing request must "Be received by the facility within 30 days from the date that the discharge notice is received by the resident or the resident’s legal representative;".

7 days' bed-hold (nursing facility) — DHSS Long Term Care Institutional Provider Specific Policy Manual § 4.5.1 and § 4.5.1.1, quoted in full above: payment "may continue for a period of not more than 7 days" provided the facility agrees to hold the bed, with DMAP reimbursement "available for only 7 days within each 30-day period."

14 days' bed-hold (ICF/IID) — the same manual, § 4.5.2 and § 4.5.2.1: payment "may continue for a period of not more than 14 days" provided the ICF/IID agrees to hold the bed, with DMAP reimbursement "available for only 14 days within each 30-day period for members residing in an ICF/IID."

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the hearing office to determine.

Pennsylvania

Grounds stated in the sources
The Department of Human Services' Long-Term Care Handbook, section 423.61, states that a facility "may discharge an individual for nonpayment of his payment towards the cost of LTC Facility Services when" the facility "has provided reasonable and appropriate notice." and "has demonstrated that it made reasonable efforts to collect the debt.", and lists alongside it that "The individual’s health improved to the point where LTC facility services are no longer needed." and "The individual’s health has deteriorated requiring care which the facility is unable to provide." — quoted in full, in the handbook's own order, in section 01. The handbook cites 42 CFR § 483.12 and 028 Pa. Code § 201.29(f) for the passage; the federal enumeration is quoted on the federal page and is not restated here, and section 01 records that the currently published text of 201.29(f) does not match the handbook's attribution.
Notice stated in the sources
The same handbook section: "When an LTC facility discharges an individual or transfers the individual to another facility, the former facility must give the individual 30 days advance written notice, and, if known, a copy of the notice to the individual’s representative." The stated exception follows: "The notice is not required for an emergency or an urgent need for medical services which the facility cannot provide." No Pennsylvania source captured for this page separately enumerates what the notice itself must state; 28 Pa. Code § 201.2(a) instead adopts the federal Subpart B requirements — which include the federal notice-content enumeration — as the Commonwealth's own licensing regulation, quoted in section 02.
Hearing office in these sources
The Bureau of Hearings and Appeals: the handbook states "The individual or the individual’s representative has the right to appeal a discharge or transfer." and that "The appeal must be received by the CAO within 30 days of the date of the facility's notice to the individual of discharge. The appeal must be filed with the Bureau of Hearings and Appeals." No form is required — "There is no specific form that an individual or the individual’s representative must complete to file an appeal." — and four items the request must contain are listed. The county assistance office sends a copy to the "Office of Long-Term Living Bureau of Provider Support P. O. Box 8025 Harrisburg, PA 17105 (717) 772-2570", and "A copy of the appeal request must also be sent to the LTC facility." No street address or filing portal for the Bureau of Hearings and Appeals itself is stated in the sources reviewed. The Department of Human Services' general fair-hearing procedure, 55 Pa. Code § 275.4, states a pendency provision for Medical Assistance generally; section 02 quotes it and records what it does and does not say about a nursing-facility discharge specifically.
Ombudsman in these sources
The Pennsylvania Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program, in the Department of Aging. As published on the Commonwealth's own service page, contact is "By Phone" — "Call the PA Long-term Care Office to connect with your local Ombudsman." at 717-783-8975 — and "By Email" at LTC-Ombudsman@pa.gov. The Department of Health's facility protocol routes discharge notices to the same address: the facility is to "e-mail notice to LTC-ombudsman@pa.gov the same day it’s issued to the resident. The State Office will distribute the notice to the appropriate local ombudsman."

LedeWhat this page holds

Lede

This page assembles what Pennsylvania's own publishers state about involuntary transfer and discharge from a nursing facility. The fullest single source is the Department of Human Services' Long-Term Care Handbook at 423.6, "Changes in Residence/Discharge/Transfers" (Updated June 11, 2025, Replacing August 22, 2019) — a Medical Assistance eligibility manual rather than a residents' guide, which is where the notice period, the appeal route and the bed-hold period are actually published. Pennsylvania's vocabulary is discharge and transfer; the appeal is filed with the Bureau of Hearings and Appeals through the county assistance office, abbreviated CAO throughout the handbook. Four publishers hold the pieces: the Department of Human Services runs the appeal, the Medical Assistance rules and the general fair-hearing procedure, the Department of Health regulates the facilities and sets the notice-routing protocol, the Pennsylvania Code publishes the licensing regulation that adopts the federal facility requirements, and the Department of Aging houses the ombudsman program.

The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on the federal page; nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

01The grounds, as Pennsylvania describes them

The grounds, as Pennsylvania describes them

The Long-Term Care Handbook, section 423.61, "LTC Facility Discharge and Transfer" (PA Department of Human Services, Updated June 11, 2025, Replacing August 22, 2019, retrieved 2026-09-06), states, citing 42 CFR § 483.12 and 028 Pa. Code § 201.29(f), that:

"An LTC facility may discharge an individual for nonpayment of his payment towards the cost of LTC Facility Services when:"

"The LTC facility has provided reasonable and appropriate notice."

"The LTC facility has demonstrated that it made reasonable efforts to collect the debt."

"The individual’s health improved to the point where LTC facility services are no longer needed."

"The individual’s health has deteriorated requiring care which the facility is unable to provide."

These four items are listed in this order, immediately under the "when:" clause quoted above, in the handbook itself; the handbook's own formatting does not make clear whether the third and fourth items are additional preconditions for a nonpayment discharge or separate, freestanding grounds unrelated to nonpayment. Reproduced in the handbook's own order and grouping; not reconciled — see finding (8) below.

The currently published text of 28 Pa. Code § 201.29(f), captured in full as source 4, reads only "[Reserved]" — the cited subsection carries no operative text matching the handbook's own attribution. See finding (7) below.

02The published process

The published process

Requesting the hearing

The handbook: "The individual or the individual’s representative has the right to appeal a discharge or transfer. There is no specific form that an individual or the individual’s representative must complete to file an appeal. However, the appeal request must contain the following:" — citing 55 Pa. Code § 275.1 — "the name of the individual on whose behalf the appeal is being filed.", "the name and address of the LTC facility involved.", "the reason for the appeal.", and "the name of the individual or the individual’s legally authorized representative filing the appeal." "The appeal must be received by the CAO within 30 days of the date of the facility's notice to the individual of discharge. The appeal must be filed with the Bureau of Hearings and Appeals. The CAO will send a copy of the appeal request to the Office of Long-Term Living (OLTL) at the following address:" — "Office of Long-Term Living Bureau of Provider Support P. O. Box 8025 Harrisburg, PA 17105 (717) 772-2570" — and "A copy of the appeal request must also be sent to the LTC facility. If the appeal involves a dispute with the recommendations of the individual’s physician, a copy must also be sent to the physician." No street address or filing portal for the Bureau of Hearings and Appeals itself is stated in the sources reviewed.

The Department of Human Services' general fair-hearing procedure, 55 Pa. Code § 275.4(a)(3)(v)(C)(I): "When an applicant/recipient responds to a written notice by requesting a hearing within the specified time period as described in § 133.4 (relating to procedures), the agency receiving the hearing request will date stamp the form upon receipt, review the complaint as required in this paragraph, and forward the appeal to the Office of Hearings and Appeals within 3 working days from the time the appeal is received from scheduling a fair hearing. In cases in which a hearing is requested within the specified time period, assistance will be continued pending the hearing decision unless the client waives continuation of benefits." This is a pendency statement about continuation of Medical Assistance payment, stated generally for the Department's fair-hearing procedure rather than specifically for a nursing-facility discharge appeal. No statement barring a facility from physically transferring or discharging a resident while an appeal is pending was found in the sources reviewed for Pennsylvania — searched: the Long-Term Care Handbook 423.6, 28 Pa. Code § 201.29, 28 Pa. Code § 201.2, and 55 Pa. Code § 275.4. Not stated in the sources reviewed. See finding (10) below.

The notice's required contents

The handbook: "When an LTC facility discharges an individual or transfers the individual to another facility, the former facility must give the individual 30 days advance written notice, and, if known, a copy of the notice to the individual’s representative." Exception: "The notice is not required for an emergency or an urgent need for medical services which the facility cannot provide." "The facility must make arrangements for the safe and orderly transfer and must prepare the individual for the transfer."

No captured Pennsylvania source separately enumerates the substantive items a discharge notice must itself contain — a reason, an effective date, appeal-rights text, or ombudsman contact information printed in the notice. 28 Pa. Code § 201.2(a), "Requirements": "The Department incorporates by reference 42 CFR Part 483, Subpart B of the Federal requirements for long-term care facilities, (relating to requirements for long-term care facilities), as licensing regulations for long-term care nursing facilities, with the exception of 42 CFR 483.1 (relating to basis and scope) and the requirements under 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart B for the transmission of data and minimum data set (MDS) reporting to the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) unless the facility is participating in the Medicare or Medical Assistance Program." This is the Commonwealth's own regulation adopting the federal Subpart B requirements — which include the federal notice-content enumeration at 42 CFR 483.15(c) — as Pennsylvania's own licensing law, rather than a state-specific enumeration; the federal text is quoted on the federal page and is not restated here. See finding (9) below.

Separately, the Department of Health's own protocol for facilities states a routing step distinct from the notice's contents: for a facility-initiated, non-emergency discharge, the facility is to "Issue a written 30-day notice of discharge to the resident and e-mail notice to LTC-ombudsman@pa.gov the same day it’s issued to the resident. The State Office will distribute the notice to the appropriate local ombudsman."

Bed-hold and return

The handbook: "When an MA LTC Services recipient is admitted to a hospital, but not officially discharged from the facility, the CAO will not take any immediate action because a recipient can be eligible for a maximum of 15 days consecutive bed hold days due to hospitalization." The handbook cites 55 Pa. Code § 1187.104(a) in the same passage; see finding (11) below on how that citation sits relative to the quoted sentence. No captured Pennsylvania source states a readmission or return guarantee following a hospital bed-hold beyond the 15-day count itself — searched: the Long-Term Care Handbook 423.6, 28 Pa. Code § 201.29, and 28 Pa. Code § 201.2. Not stated in the sources reviewed.

The three rows above hoist the questions a facility's counsel answers in the first fifteen minutes. They are quotations from Pennsylvania's published sources, not a determination that any of them applies to any situation.

Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

30 days' advance notice — the handbook: "the former facility must give the individual 30 days advance written notice", with the emergency exception quoted above.

30 days to file an appeal — the handbook: "The appeal must be received by the CAO within 30 days of the date of the facility's notice to the individual of discharge."

15 consecutive bed-hold days — the handbook: "a recipient can be eligible for a maximum of 15 days consecutive bed hold days due to hospitalization."

3 working days — the handbook: an LTC facility "will send a Long-Term Care Admissions and Discharge Transmittal (MA 103) to CAO staff within three working days of a transfer or discharge from the facility."

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the hearing office to determine.

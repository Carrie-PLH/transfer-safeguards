Massachusetts

Grounds stated in the sources
No enumeration of permissible bases appears in any captured Massachusetts source. The Division of Medical Assistance's summary of residents' rights, LTC-013 (Rev. 01/01), states the right to notice and the right to appeal but states no grounds; the two regulations that would carry them, 130 CMR 456.000 and 130 CMR 610.000, are capture-pending. The federal enumeration is quoted on [the federal page](../federal.html) and is not restated here. Not stated in the sources reviewed.
Notice stated in the sources
LTC-013: "You have the right to receive written notice if the nursing facility intends to transfer" "or discharge you to another unit within the facility, to another nursing facility, to a" "hospital or other institution, or to the community." No advance-notice period is stated in the captured sources. The same document states a separate right on room changes — to reasonable accommodation of needs and preferences "and to receive notice before your room or" "roommate in the facility is changed." — and a right "to refuse a transfer to another room within the facility if the" "purpose of the transfer is based upon your eligibility or ineligibility for Medicare" "coverage."
Hearing office in these sources
The Office of Medicaid, Board of Hearings. LTC-013 states the right "to appeal the" "transfer or discharge by requesting a fair hearing before the Division of Medical" "Assistance (the state Medicaid agency)." and that the facility "must provide you with" "the address to which you must send the request for a fair hearing and must assist" "you with requesting a hearing." MassHealth publishes the routes — by mail or in person at "Office of Medicaid, Board of Hearings" "1 Enterprise Drive, 3rd Floor" "Quincy, MA 02171", by fax to "1-617-887-8797", by email to boh-fairhearingsrequest@mass.gov, and by telephone through the MassHealth Customer Service Center at "(800) 841-2900", with the instruction "Please do not call the Board of Hearings to file a fair hearing request." The Board's published mailing address in the same page's contact block is different: "100 Hancock Street, 6th Floor, Quincy, MA 02171". Its telephone numbers are "Main: (617) 847-1200" and "Toll free: (800) 655-0338".
Ombudsman in these sources
The Long-Term Care Ombudsman, in the Executive Office of Aging & Independence. The Commonwealth publishes "Long-Term Care Ombudsman and Assisted Living Ombudsman: 617-222-7495" and a general program line, "Contact an Ombudsman Program: (617) 727-7750/711 MassRelay/TTY", with the office at "One Ashburton Place, 10th floor, Boston, MA 02108" and MassOptions at "800-243-4636".

LedeWhat this page holds

Lede

This page assembles what Massachusetts's own publishers state about transfer and discharge from a nursing facility. It is a baseline page, and a thin one: the docket rows and the source map are built, and two of the four docket questions come back as absences the change log records. The fullest captured source is the Division of Medical Assistance's summary of residents' rights, LTC-013 (Rev. 01/01) — a document whose revision line reads 2001 and which is what the Commonwealth still publishes. Massachusetts's vocabulary is transfer or discharge; the appeal is a fair hearing before the Board of Hearings, the unit inside MassHealth that hears Medicaid appeals generally rather than an office dedicated to discharge.

The regulations that would state the grounds and the notice period — 130 CMR 456.000 and 130 CMR 610.000 — refused capture, so this page states what it holds and no more. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

04Change log

Change log

2026-08-30 — Baseline page built from the first Massachusetts packet: docket, lede, source map, change log. Not yet captured or written: section 01 (the grounds, as Massachusetts describes them) and section 02 (the published process, including the notice's required contents, bed-hold and return, and the notice-periods register). Findings: (1) mass.gov returns HTTP 403 to curl for both document downloads and regulation pages; all three sources were captured through the session's fetch tool, and the two regulations that would state the grounds and the notice period — 130 CMR 456.000 and 130 CMR 610.000 — were not captured at all. The grounds row and the notice period therefore stand as absences. (2) No advance-notice period appears in any captured Massachusetts source, and none is supplied from federal law or another state. (3) MassHealth publishes two different addresses for the Board of Hearings on the same page: the filing instructions say to mail or deliver to 1 Enterprise Drive, 3rd Floor, Quincy, and the contact block at the foot gives the mailing address as 100 Hancock Street, 6th Floor, Quincy. Both are quoted as published, reconciled nowhere. (4) The sixty-day filing window MassHealth states — "The Board of Hearings must receive your completed, signed request within 60 calendar days from the date you received the notice of the MassHealth agency’s action." — is stated for MassHealth agency actions generally; whether it is the window for a facility-initiated transfer or discharge appeal is not stated on the page captured, and this page does not assume it. (5) The residents' rights summary a facility is required to give carries a revision line of 01/01; that is the newest version the Commonwealth publishes at this URL. (6) The page's ombudsman row carries two program numbers because the Commonwealth publishes two — a program line and a long-term care line. Capture pending: per the source map.

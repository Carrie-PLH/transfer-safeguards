South Carolina

Grounds stated in the sources
S.C. Code § 44-81-40(D): "A resident may be transferred or discharged only for medical reasons, for the welfare of the resident or for the welfare of other residents of the facility, or for nonpayment and must be given written notice of not less than thirty days, except that when the health, safety, or welfare of other residents of the facility would be endangered by the thirty-day notice requirement, the time for giving notice must be that which is practicable under the circumstances." Three grounds, stated in one sentence with the notice period attached. No regulation enumerating them further was captured.
Notice stated in the sources
The same sentence carries the period: written notice of not less than thirty days, shortened to what is practicable where the health, safety or welfare of other residents would be endangered by the thirty-day requirement. A move inside the building has its own line: "Each resident must be given written notice before the resident's room or roommate in the facility is changed." No statement of what the notice must contain appears in the captured South Carolina sources.
Hearing office in these sources
The South Carolina Department of Health and Human Services' Office of Appeals and Hearings, the state's general Medicaid fair-hearing forum — not a route the department created specifically for nursing-home transfer or discharge, but one its own materials state reaches exactly this situation: "You receive notice that the nursing home where you reside plans to discharge or transfer you against your wishes." is listed among the stated occasions for an appeal. Filing is by the department's general "Other Appeals" route: online, by fax to "(803) 255-8206", by mail to "Office of Appeals and Hearings" "PO Box 8206" "Columbia, SC 29202", or by email to "appeals@scdhhs.gov". The Bill of Rights, separately, states only facility-level procedures: "Each facility shall establish grievance procedures to be exercised by or on behalf of the resident to enforce the rights provided by this act." — with "The department shall review and approve these grievance procedures annually. This act is enforced by the department." Neither the Bill of Rights nor the nursing home licensing regulation R.60-17 (captured and searched in the 2026-08-30 pass) states the Office of Appeals and Hearings route itself; that statement comes from the department's own appeals pages, found and captured in this pass. See section 02 for the deadlines, the pendency provision, and the further appeal to the Administrative Law Court, all as the department states them.
Ombudsman in these sources
The Long Term Care Ombudsman Program, in the South Carolina Department on Aging, which lists "Improper transfer or discharge of a resident" among the issues it handles. Its published contact: "please call the SCDOA Long Term Care Ombudsman at 1-800-868-9095.", with "Local: (803) 734-9900", "Fax: (803) 734-9886" and the office at "1301 Gervais Street, Suite 350, Columbia, SC 29201".

LedeWhat this page holds

Lede

This page assembles what South Carolina's own publishers state about transfer and discharge from a long-term care facility. The fullest single source for the grounds and the notice is Chapter 81 of Title 44, the Bill of Rights for Residents of Long-Term Care Facilities, whose § 44-81-40(D) states both in a single sentence; the fullest source for the hearing route is the Department of Health and Human Services' own Office of Appeals and Hearings pages. South Carolina's vocabulary is transferred or discharged; the chapter's own enforcement runs through facility grievance procedures that the department reviews and approves annually, and it is a separate, general Medicaid fair-hearing apparatus — not the chapter, and not a route built specifically for this situation — that the department's own materials state reaches a nursing-home transfer or discharge notice.

Chapter 81 names no hearing office of its own. What answers that row on this page is the department's general appeals forum, found by searching the department's own site rather than by a link from Chapter 81 or from the ombudsman program page, neither of which mentions it. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on the federal page; nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

01The grounds, as South Carolina describes them

The grounds, as South Carolina describes them

S.C. Code § 44-81-40(D), part of the Bill of Rights for Residents of Long-Term Care Facilities (South Carolina Code of Laws, Title 44 Chapter 81, retrieved 2026-09-18), states the grounds and the notice period in the same sentence: "A resident may be transferred or discharged only for medical reasons, for the welfare of the resident or for the welfare of other residents of the facility, or for nonpayment and must be given written notice of not less than thirty days, except that when the health, safety, or welfare of other residents of the facility would be endangered by the thirty-day notice requirement, the time for giving notice must be that which is practicable under the circumstances." Four grounds by the sentence's own commas: medical reasons; the resident's welfare; the welfare of other residents; nonpayment. No further regulation enumerating or elaborating them was captured; the nursing home licensing regulation R.60-17 was searched in the 2026-08-30 pass and carries none. The same subsection adds one further notice obligation, for a different kind of move: "Each resident must be given written notice before the resident's room or roommate in the facility is changed."

02The published process

The published process

Requesting the hearing

No captured South Carolina statute or regulation creates a hearing specific to an involuntary transfer or discharge; the Bill of Rights routes enforcement through the facility's own grievance procedure, § 44-81-60: "Each facility shall establish grievance procedures to be exercised by or on behalf of the resident to enforce the rights provided by this act. The department shall review and approve these grievance procedures annually. This act is enforced by the department." What does exist, and was not previously captured, is the Department of Health and Human Services' general Medicaid fair-hearing forum. Its "When can I appeal?" list names this situation directly: "You receive notice that the nursing home where you reside plans to discharge or transfer you against your wishes." is one of the stated occasions for an appeal (File an Appeal, SCDHHS Office of Appeals and Hearings, retrieved 2026-09-18).

Filing route, stated for this category of appeal ("For other appeals" — the department's Eligibility Appeals route is a separate, narrower category for a denial of an initial application): "please state what and why you are appealing (why you think the decision made or action taken is wrong). Please include a copy of the notice or denial you received with your letter and be sure to include complete contact information for you and your representative (if you have one). Please submit the appeal one of the following ways:" — "Submit your appeal online." — "Fax to (803) 255-8206" — mail to "Office of Appeals and Hearings" "PO Box 8206" "Columbia, SC 29202" — or "Email your appeal to appeals@scdhhs.gov". General guidance on timing: "You should carefully read the notice or denial you receive. It will contain instructions on how long you have to appeal. Some notices will give you thirty (30) days from the date of the notice. Other notices give you thirty (30) days from the day you receive the notice." The department states no single, universal deadline for this category of appeal separate from what the individual notice itself states.

On continuing in place, the department states a request-based provision rather than an automatic stay: "New services cannot be started. However, you may request continued benefits or services if you do so within ten days (10) of the date on the notice. Your services or benefits will continue during the appeal process. Any disputed benefits or services you receive during the appeal process may have to be paid back if the hearing officer rules the agency, MCO, or other party making the decision was correct." The hearing itself: "A fair hearing is an in-person proceeding conducted by a hearing officer of the Office of Appeals and Hearings." held, ordinarily, at "Jefferson Square, 1801 Main Street, Columbia, SC 29201", though "hearings may be scheduled for county eligibility or Community Long Term Care (CLTC) offices or other locations." On timing: "Ordinarily, member appeals should be concluded within 90 days of filing." A further appeal follows: "You may appeal to the Administrative Law Court. Directions on how to contact and begin an appeal to the ALC are contained within the cover letter sent with the hearing officer's order. Please be aware that appeals must be filed with the ALC within thirty (30) days of the receipt of the hearing officer's order." The Office of Appeals and Hearings itself: "(803)898-2600 or (800)763-9087."

The notice's required contents

No captured South Carolina statute or regulation states what a facility's transfer or discharge notice must contain. § 44-81-40(D) states only the notice period, quoted above, and § 44-81-40(A) states a separate, general obligation about residents' rights notices rather than transfer notices: "Each resident or the resident's representative must be given by the facility a written and oral explanation of the rights, grievance procedures, and enforcement provisions of this chapter before or at the time of admission to a long-term care facility." R.60-17 was searched in the 2026-08-30 pass and carries no notice-contents provision; the department's appeals materials, read in this pass, tell a resident to act on whatever the notice states ("You should carefully read the notice or denial you receive. It will contain instructions on how to appeal.") without stating what the nursing home's notice itself is required to say. That absence is stated rather than filled from another state or from the federal page.

Bed-hold and return

§ 44-81-40(B)(2) ties bed-hold to the facility's own refund policy rather than stating a day count directly: "the facility's refund policy which must be adopted by each facility and which must be based upon the actual number of days a resident was in the facility and any reasonable number of bed-hold days, except when the provisions of subsection (E) apply." The statute requires a policy and requires it to be reasonable; it does not itself state how many bed-hold days are reasonable, and no further first-party South Carolina source stating a specific number was found. Searched in this pass and carrying no bed-hold provision: the Nursing Facility Services Provider Manual, SCDHHS's own current provider billing manual (retrieved and read in full 2026-09-18, no mention of a reserved-bed, bed-hold, or leave-of-absence policy anywhere in the document); a general search for a published SCDHHS Medicaid bed-hold or reserved-bed policy also returned nothing first-party. That absence is stated rather than filled from another state or from the federal page. (Subsection (E), which sets a fourteen-day notice and a charge provision for a resident's voluntary relocation from a community residential care facility, is a different facility type and a different, resident-initiated scenario from the involuntary transfer or discharge this page covers, and is not quoted here.)

Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

Thirty days' notice of transfer or discharge, with a practicability exception — § 44-81-40(D): "written notice of not less than thirty days, except that when the health, safety, or welfare of other residents of the facility would be endangered by the thirty-day notice requirement, the time for giving notice must be that which is practicable under the circumstances."

Ten days to request continued benefits or services during an appeal — SCDHHS: "you may request continued benefits or services if you do so within ten days (10) of the date on the notice."

Ninety days, ordinarily, for a member appeal to conclude — SCDHHS: "Ordinarily, member appeals should be concluded within 90 days of filing."

Thirty days to appeal the hearing officer's order to the Administrative Law Court — SCDHHS: "appeals must be filed with the ALC within thirty (30) days of the receipt of the hearing officer's order."

No single stated deadline to first request the fair hearing itself — the department directs a resident to the individual notice: "Some notices will give you thirty (30) days from the date of the notice. Other notices give you thirty (30) days from the day you receive the notice."

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the hearing office or relevant state process to determine.

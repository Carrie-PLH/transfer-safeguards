New Jersey

Grounds stated in the sources
The Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman's resident-rights page states the right "To be transferred or discharged only for one or more of the following reasons:" — "In an emergency, with notification of your physician and your next of kin or guardian or if the facility closes."; "For medical reasons, to protect your welfare or the welfare of others or to comply with your advance directive."; "For nonpayment of fees, in situations not prohibited by law."; and "Your health has improved and you no longer require nursing home care." The same office's guidance to facilities sets out a six-item list keyed to the federal regulation and states: "All transfer and discharge notices must specifically cite one of the six above reasons. Other justifications, such as behavioral problems or failure to follow facility policies, are not reasons under federal law."
Notice stated in the sources
The resident-rights page states the right "To receive notice at least 30 days in advance when the nursing home requests your transfer or discharge, except in an emergency. That notice should provide appeal information and a copy should be sent to LTCO." The office's guidance states the same period and adds a longer one for closures: "The notice must include the effective date when the transfer/discharge is to be carried out and must be issued at" "least 30 days prior to that date. When the facility is closing, the notice must be issued at least 60 days prior to the" "proposed transfer/discharge date. Notice to the resident or the resident’s representative is still required in" "emergent circumstances." On destination, the guidance states: "Vague language such as “to other facility” or “home” is not sufficient."
Hearing office in these sources
The Fair Hearing Unit of the State Medicaid agency. The ombudsman's guidance to facilities states: "Residents, regardless of payor source, have the right under federal law to appeal transfer/discharge notices to the" "State Medicaid agency. You must include contact information for the Fair Hearing Unit of the State Medicaid" "agency for this purpose." — and publishes the address as "Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services" "Fair Hearing Unit" "P.O. Box 712" "Trenton, NJ 08625" "(609) 588-2655". No filing deadline is stated in the sources reviewed, and the Division's own statement of the route was not captured; this contact is the ombudsman office's publication of it, not the Division's. The guidance also states what the ombudsman office is not: "Notices should never list the LTCO as an appeals agency."
Ombudsman in these sources
The Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, which publishes its own contact as "Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman" "P.O. Box 852" "Trenton, NJ 08625-0852" "(877) 582-6995" "Fax: (609) 943-3479" "mandatoryreports@LTCO.nj.gov". The resident-rights page gives the same number: "The LTCO can be contacted at 1-877-582-6995."

LedeWhat this page holds

Lede

This page assembles what New Jersey's own publishers state about involuntary transfer and discharge from a nursing facility. It is a baseline page: the docket rows and the source map are built; the grounds section and the published-process section are not yet written, as the change log records. Both captured sources come from one publisher — the Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, a state office that publishes both a residents' rights page and a written notice-requirements guidance for facilities. New Jersey's vocabulary is transfer or discharge; the appeal is a fair hearing before the State Medicaid agency's Fair Hearing Unit.

That single-publisher shape is itself the finding here: the office that runs the appeals is the Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services, and its own statement of the route for these appeals was not captured, so the hearing row carries the ombudsman office's publication of the Division's contact and says so. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

Change log

2026-08-30 — Baseline page built from the first New Jersey packet: docket, lede, source map, change log. Not yet captured or written: section 01 (the grounds, as New Jersey describes them) and section 02 (the published process, including the notice's required contents in full, bed-hold and return, and the notice-periods register). Findings: (1) Both captured sources come from the same publisher, the Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman; the docket says so, and the hearing row states that the Fair Hearing Unit's contact is that office's publication of it rather than the Division's own. (2) The Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services' Medicaid hearings page returned HTTP 404 at the path tried, and no other DMAHS statement of the transfer and discharge appeal route was captured; the absence is stated rather than filled. (3) No filing deadline for a transfer or discharge fair hearing is stated in either captured source. (4) The two sources state the grounds differently: the resident-rights page gives a four-item list in the office's own words, and the facilities guidance gives a six-item list keyed to 42 C.F.R. § 483.15. Both stand as published, reconciled nowhere, and the federal enumeration stays on the federal page. (5) The facilities guidance states an instruction to facilities that bears directly on what a resident's notice should look like: "Notices should never list the LTCO as an appeals agency." (6) Neither source states a New Jersey bed-hold period; the resident-rights page refers only to being informed of the facility's own bed-hold policy on transfer to a hospital, and no period is supplied from any other source.

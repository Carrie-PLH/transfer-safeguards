New Jersey

Grounds stated in the sources
The Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman's resident-rights page states the right "To be transferred or discharged only for one or more of the following reasons:" — "In an emergency, with notification of your physician and your next of kin or guardian or if the facility closes."; "For medical reasons, to protect your welfare or the welfare of others or to comply with your advance directive."; "For nonpayment of fees, in situations not prohibited by law."; and "Your health has improved and you no longer require nursing home care." The same page separately states: "Transfers to another facility cannot be solely for the convenience of the facility – and LTCO must be notified." The office's guidance to facilities sets out a six-item list and states: "All transfer and discharge notices must specifically cite one of the six above reasons. Other justifications, such as behavioral problems or failure to follow facility policies, are not reasons under federal law." Both lists, and the pendency and documentation statements next to them, are quoted in full in section 01.
Notice stated in the sources
The resident-rights page states the right "To receive notice at least 30 days in advance when the nursing home requests your transfer or discharge, except in an emergency. That notice should provide appeal information and a copy should be sent to LTCO." The office's guidance states the same period and adds a longer one for closures: "The notice must include the effective date when the transfer/discharge is to be carried out and must be issued at" "least 30 days prior to that date. When the facility is closing, the notice must be issued at least 60 days prior to the" "proposed transfer/discharge date. Notice to the resident or the resident’s representative is still required in" "emergent circumstances." On destination, the guidance states: "Vague language such as “to other facility” or “home” is not sufficient." The same guidance publishes a full "Summary of Notice Requirements" list and delivery, disability-advocacy-contact, and notice-update requirements, quoted in section 02.

LedeWhat this page holds

Lede

This page assembles what New Jersey's own publishers state about involuntary transfer and discharge from a nursing facility. Both captured sources come from one publisher — the Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, a state office that publishes both a residents' rights page and a seven-part written notice-requirements guidance for facilities, "Notice Requirements for Transfer/Discharge of a Long-Term Care Resident" — the fuller of the two and the basis for most of section 02. New Jersey's vocabulary is transfer or discharge; the appeal is a fair hearing before the State Medicaid agency's Fair Hearing Unit.

That single-publisher shape is itself a finding, carried from the baseline: the office that runs the appeals is the Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services, and its own statement of the route for these appeals was not captured, so the hearing row carries the ombudsman office's publication of the Division's contact and says so. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on the federal page; nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

01The grounds, as New Jersey describes them

The grounds, as New Jersey describes them

The resident-rights page (NJ Long-Term Care Ombudsman, Last Updated: Thursday, 05/15/25, retrieved 2026-09-06), under "Discharges and Transfers", states the right:

"To be transferred or discharged only for one or more of the following reasons:"

"In an emergency, with notification of your physician and your next of kin or guardian or if the facility closes."

"For medical reasons, to protect your welfare or the welfare of others or to comply with your advance directive."

"For nonpayment of fees, in situations not prohibited by law."

"Your health has improved and you no longer require nursing home care."

The same page states, in the same section: "Transfers to another facility cannot be solely for the convenience of the facility – and LTCO must be notified."

The office's facilities guidance, "Notice Requirements for Transfer/Discharge of a Long-Term Care Resident" (NJ Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, PDF, retrieved 2026-09-06), states the allowable-reason rule in fuller, numbered form: "The facility must permit each resident to remain in the facility, and not transfer or discharge the resident from the facility unless:"

"1. The transfer or discharge is necessary for the resident's welfare and the resident's needs cannot be met in the facility pursuant to 42 C.F.R. § 483.15 (a)(6);"

"2. The transfer or discharge is appropriate because the resident's health has improved sufficiently so the resident no longer needs the services provided by the facility;"

"3. The safety of individuals in the facility is endangered due to the clinical or behavioral status of the resident;"

"4. The health of individuals in the facility would otherwise be endangered;"

"5. The resident has failed, after reasonable and appropriate notice, to pay for (or to have paid under Medicare or Medicaid) a stay at the facility. Non-payment applies if the resident does not submit the necessary paperwork for third party payment or after the third party, including Medicare or Medicaid, denies the claim and the resident refuses to pay for his or her stay."

"6. The facility closes."

Immediately after the list, the guidance states the pendency rule and a documentation duty, quoted in full under Requesting the hearing in section 02, and then: "All transfer and discharge notices must specifically cite one of the six above reasons. Other justifications, such as behavioral problems or failure to follow facility policies, are not reasons under federal law." It also states a duty that runs alongside any transfer or discharge, whatever the ground: "the facility must ensure that the transfer or discharge is documented in the resident's medical record and appropriate information is communicated to the receiving health care institution or provider."

The resident-rights page's four-item list and the guidance's six-item list are not worded alike; both stand as published, reconciled nowhere — see finding (8) in the change log. The guidance's own citations (42 C.F.R. § 483.15) are the state office's citations for what it tells New Jersey facilities to do; the federal enumeration itself is quoted separately on the federal page and is not restated here.

02The published process

The published process

Requesting the hearing

The facilities guidance states the pendency rule immediately under the allowable-reason list: "A facility may not transfer or discharge a resident while an appeal is pending, when a resident exercises his or her right to appeal a transfer or discharge notice from the facility, unless the failure to discharge or transfer would endanger the health or safety of the resident or other individuals in the facility. The facility must also be sure to document the danger that failure to transfer or discharge would pose." It states the appeal route: "Residents, regardless of payor source, have the right under federal law to appeal transfer/discharge notices to the State Medicaid agency. You must include contact information for the Fair Hearing Unit of the State Medicaid agency for this purpose." — publishing the address as "Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services" "Fair Hearing Unit" "P.O. Box 712" "Trenton, NJ 08625" "(609) 588-2655". No filing deadline, filing form, or online portal is stated in the sources reviewed — searched: both captured sources in full. The guidance separately states what the ombudsman office's own role is not: "Notices should never list the LTCO as an appeals agency. The LTCO has no involvement in facility decisions to involuntarily remove residents, nor does the LTCO have a process for resident appeals." This contact and this pendency statement are the ombudsman office's own publication of the Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services' process; the Division's own first-party statement of its fair-hearing procedure and filing deadline was not reached — searched: the Division's public web pages under nj.gov/humanservices/dmahs, which state no transfer-or-discharge fair-hearing procedure page at the addresses tried.

The notice's required contents

The facilities guidance states, in a closing "Summary of Notice Requirements": "the notice requirements for transfer/discharge of a resident from a nursing facility must contain:" "The reason for transfer or discharge;" "The effective date;" "The location to which the resident is transferred or discharged;" "A statement of the resident's appeal rights, including contact information for the appeal agency, information on how to obtain an appeal form and assistance in completing the form and submitting the appeal hearing request;" and "Contact information for the NJ LTCO or Disability Rights NJ and appeals information for the Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services Fair Hearing Unit."

The body of the guidance states each item at greater length. On destination: "The notice must identify the location to which the resident will be transferred or discharged. This too, must be specific. Vague language such as “to other facility” or “home” is not sufficient." On the ombudsman contact the notice must carry: "The notice must include the name, telephone number and address of the LTCO." — published as "Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman" "P.O. Box 852" "Trenton, NJ 08625-0852" "(877) 582-6995" "Fax: (609) 943-3479" "mandatoryreports@LTCO.nj.gov". Where a resident has a disability, an added contact is required: "If the resident has an intellectual or developmental disability or a mental illness, the notice must also include the mailing and email address and telephone number of the agency responsible for protection and advocacy under federal law." — named as "Disability Rights New Jersey" at "210 South Broad Street, 3rd Floor" "Trenton, New Jersey 08608" "(609) 292-9742 or (800) 922-7233 in NJ Only" "(609) 777-0187 (Fax)" "(609) 633-7106 (TTY)" "advocate@drnj.org". On delivery: "The notice of the transfer/discharge must be given to the resident or the resident's representative(s). The notice must contain the reasons for the move in writing and in a language and manner they understand." and "a copy of the notice must be sent to the LTCO when the transfer/discharge is initiated by the facility." The guidance states plainly that giving the notice only to family is not enough: "This is not legally sufficient and, more importantly, does not alert the very person whose rights are impacted by the proposed transfer or discharge." On a later change: "If the information in the notice changes prior to effecting the transfer or discharge, the facility must update the recipients of the notice as soon as practicable once the updated information becomes available."

Bed-hold and return

The resident-rights page states the right "To be informed of the facility's bed-hold policy when you are transferred to an acute care or psychiatric hospital." No period is stated for that policy, and no other first-party statement of a bed-hold day count was found in the sources reviewed — searched: both captured sources in full. Not stated in the sources reviewed. Separately, for a resident receiving Medicaid, the facilities guidance states a permission step that precedes any relocation rather than a bed-hold period: "If the resident is receiving Medicaid, you must first contact your local Office of Community Choice Options (OCCO) for permission to relocate him or her. The New Jersey Department of Health's regulations outlining that procedure can be found at N.J.A.C. 8:85-1.10. If OCCO grants its permission to transfer the resident, only then can you issue a 30-day notice to relocate the resident to another appropriate placement." N.J.A.C. 8:85-1.10 itself was not reached: New Jersey's Administrative Code is published through a LexisNexis Advance portal (linked from the Department of Health's own rules page) that returned a login-gated page to the transports available for this pass, rather than the rule text — see finding (9).

The three rows above hoist the questions a facility's counsel answers in the first fifteen minutes. They are quotations from New Jersey's published sources, not a determination that any of them applies to any situation.

Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

30 days' advance notice — the resident-rights page: "at least 30 days in advance when the nursing home requests your transfer or discharge, except in an emergency"; the facilities guidance: "must be issued at least 30 days prior to that date", referring to the effective date in the notice.

60 days for a closure — the facilities guidance: "When the facility is closing, the notice must be issued at least 60 days prior to the proposed transfer/discharge date."

30 days for a Medicaid relocation after OCCO permission — the facilities guidance: "only then can you issue a 30-day notice to relocate the resident to another appropriate placement."

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the hearing office to determine.

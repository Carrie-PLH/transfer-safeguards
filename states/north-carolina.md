# North Carolina

## Docket

**Grounds stated in the sources.** N.C.G.S. § 131E-117(15) states the patient's right "To not be transferred or discharged from a facility except for medical" "reasons, the patient's own or other patients' welfare, nonpayment for the stay," "or when the transfer or discharge is mandated under Title XVIII (Medicare)" "or Title XIX (Medicaid) of the Social Security Act." NC Medicaid's prescribed notice form states a different list under a different authority: "Under federal law 42 CFR §483.15, you may only be transferred or discharged from this nursing facility for one of the following" "reasons:" — six checkboxes follow. Both are shown as published; the federal enumeration is assembled on [the federal page](../federal.html).

**Notice stated in the sources.** The statute states the patient "shall be" "given at least five days' advance notice to ensure orderly transfer or" "discharge, unless the attending physician orders immediate transfer, and" "these actions, and the reasons for them, shall be documented in the patient's" "medical record". No 30-day period appears in the captured North Carolina sources. The state's notice form, NC Medicaid-9050 (6/2026), sets out the fields the notice must carry — date of notice, resident and facility, "DATE OF TRANSFER/DISCHARGE", the checked reason, who else was notified, the destination, appeal rights, and the regional ombudsman's contact, with a box recording whether the "Facility sent Ombudsman a copy of the Notice".

**Hearing office in these sources.** The NC Medicaid Hearing Office, which "Hears appeals by long-term care residents who are being transferred or discharged from an adult care home or skilled nursing facility" and "conducts formal hearings for residents appealing a notice of discharge from a Skilled Nursing Facility. (10A NCAC 22H.0200)". Its published contact is "NC Medicaid Hearing Office" "919-814-0090" "Fax: 984-687-9584" "Medicaid.Hearings@dhhs.nc.gov". The notice form states the deadline: "You have the right to appeal this transfer/discharge to the DHHS Hearing Office WITHIN 11 CALENDAR DAYS of the date of" "this notice if you want to continue to stay at this facility." — and that the request "must be received by the hearing officer no later than the 11 th calendar day or your right to appeal is" "waived." It adds: "The appeal will be at no cost to you or your representative." and that a resident wishing to review their medical record must be allowed to see it "no later than five working days prior to the" "hearing."

**Ombudsman in these sources.** The Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program, which "consists of an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman and 16 Offices of the Regional Long-Term Care Ombudsman that are housed in Area Agencies on Aging." No statewide telephone number for the program is published on the page reviewed; access runs through the region, and the department directs the reader to "Find information for your county". The notice form leaves the regional ombudsman's name, email, address and phone as blanks the facility fills in.

## Lede

This page assembles what North Carolina's own publishers state about involuntary transfer and discharge from a nursing facility. There is no single fullest source here. The patient's rights statute, § 131E-117, states the grounds and a five-day notice; NC Medicaid's prescribed notice form, NC Medicaid-9050 (6/2026), states a six-reason federal list and an eleven-day appeal deadline; NC Medicaid's appeals page publishes the hearing office; and NC Medicaid's Nursing Facilities Clinical Coverage Policy No. 2B-1 states the Medicaid therapeutic-leave allowance and the bed-hold arrangement. North Carolina's vocabulary is *transfer or discharge*, and the statute calls the resident a *patient*; the appeal goes to the Medicaid hearing office rather than to the licensing agency.

The eleven-day deadline is the shortest appeal clock this project has recorded so far, and it runs from the date of the notice rather than from receipt. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

## 01 — The grounds, as North Carolina describes them

N.C.G.S. § 131E-117(15), "Declaration of patient's rights" ([North Carolina General Assembly (PDF)](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/BySection/Chapter_131E/GS_131E-117.pdf), no date stated on the document, retrieved 2026-09-13), states the patient's right:

"To not be transferred or discharged from a facility except for medical" "reasons, the patient's own or other patients' welfare, nonpayment for the stay," "or when the transfer or discharge is mandated under Title XVIII (Medicare)" "or Title XIX (Medicaid) of the Social Security Act."

Separately, NC Medicaid's prescribed notice form, NC Medicaid-9050 (6/2026), states its own six-reason list under federal rather than state authority: "Under federal law 42 CFR §483.15, you may only be transferred or discharged from this nursing facility for one of the following" "reasons:" —

"It is necessary for your welfare and your needs cannot be met in this facility;"

"Your health has improved sufficiently so that you no longer need the services provided by this facility;"

"The safety of individuals in this facility is endangered due to the clinical or behavioral status of the resident;"

"The health of individuals in this facility would otherwise be endangered;"

"You have failed, after reasonable and appropriate notice, to pay for (or to have paid under Medicare or Medicaid) a stay at this" "facility; or"

"The facility ceases to operate."

The two lists are shown as published, reconciled nowhere: the statute states its own four-part ground under state law, and the document a resident actually receives states the federal six-part enumeration instead. The federal enumeration itself is assembled on [the federal page](../federal.html).

## 02 — The published process

### Requesting the hearing

The notice form's appeal-rights paragraph: "You have the right to appeal this transfer/discharge to the DHHS Hearing Office WITHIN 11 CALENDAR DAYS of the date of" "this notice if you want to continue to stay at this facility." "The request for" "an appeal (see attached form) must be received by the hearing officer no later than the 11 th calendar day or your right to appeal is" "waived." "The appeal will be at no cost to you or your representative." The hearing office itself, per NC Medicaid's appeals page: "The Hearings Office conducts formal hearings for residents appealing a notice of discharge from a Skilled Nursing Facility. (10A NCAC 22H.0200)". Its published contact is "NC Medicaid Hearing Office" "919-814-0090" "Fax: 984-687-9584" "Medicaid.Hearings@dhhs.nc.gov". The page links a "Nursing Home Hearing Request Form" as the filing route, but that form itself (NC Medicaid-9051) returned HTTP 404 at the address found and is capture-pending; no street address for filing, and no statement that an appeal stays the transfer or discharge while pending, appears in the sources reviewed. Not stated in the sources reviewed.

### The notice's required contents

NC Medicaid-9050 (6/2026) is itself the notice, and its own fields are what it requires: "DATE OF NOTICE", resident and facility identification, "DATE OF TRANSFER/DISCHARGE", the checked federal reason, "In addition to notifying you (i.e. the resident) of this transfer/discharge," a named representative "has also been" "notified", and the destination — "NAME OF FACILITY/LOCATION" and address. Its appeal-rights block, quoted above in full, is followed by an ombudsman block: "You may wish to contact your regional Long Term Care Ombudsman for help in mediation with the facility or for assistance in" "obtaining free legal services, if qualified." The form leaves the regional ombudsman's "NAME", "EMAIL", "ADDRESS" and "PHONE" as blanks and carries a checkbox for "Facility sent Ombudsman a copy of the Notice". Where "mentally ill or developmentally disabled" applies, the form directs the reader to "DISABILITY" "RIGHTS NORTH CAROLINA, 3724 National Drive, Suite 100, Raleigh, NC 27612. Telephone number: (919) 856-2195 or 1-" "877-235-4210 or TTY 1-888-268-5535". No separate first-party statement of the notice's required contents exists apart from the form itself in the sources reviewed; the statute's own notice clause states only the five-day period, quoted below.

### Bed-hold and return

NC Medicaid's Nursing Facilities Clinical Coverage Policy No. 2B-1 (Amended Date: October 1, 2015), subsection 5.7, "Therapeutic Leave": "Therapeutic leave must be provided in accordance with 10A NCAC 22O.0409." "Each Medicaid eligible resident who is occupying a nursing facility bed for which the" "N.C. Medicaid program is then paying reimbursement shall be entitled to take up to" "60 days of therapeutic leave in any calendar year from any such bed, without the" "facility’s suffering any loss of reimbursement during the period of leave." The leave "must be for therapeutic purposes only, and must be ordered" "by the resident's attending physician", and "Facilities must reserve a therapeutically absent resident's bed for him or her, and are" "prohibited from deriving any Medicaid revenue for that resident other than the" "reimbursement for that bed during the period of absence." "No more than 15 consecutive therapeutic leave days may be taken without approval" "of the DMA."

The same policy's subsection 7.4, "Bed Holds", addresses hospitalization separately and states there is no Medicaid bed-hold program at all: "In accordance with 42 CFR 483.12(b), when a resident must be hospitalized, the resident" "or responsible party may arrange to reserve the resident’s bed in the nursing facility." "Medicaid has no provision for bed hold; it is a private agreement between the resident or" "responsible party and the nursing facility. The admission contract must state the charge" "for bed hold and the resident must be notified of any changes as they are made." No first-party statement of a Medicaid hospital bed-hold payment (as distinct from the private admission-contract arrangement just quoted) was found in the sources reviewed (searched: this policy's own bed-hold and therapeutic-leave subsections, the statute, the notice form, and the appeals page). Not stated in the sources reviewed. On return after a hearing, no first-party statement of a readmission right was captured; the federal bed-hold floor is quoted on [the federal page](../federal.html) and is not restated here.

### Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

Five days' advance notice — § 131E-117(15): the patient "shall be" "given at least five days' advance notice to ensure orderly transfer or" "discharge, unless the attending physician orders immediate transfer".

Eleven calendar days to appeal — the notice form, quoted above in full: the appeal must reach the hearing officer "WITHIN 11 CALENDAR DAYS of the date of" "this notice", and a late request means "your right to appeal is" "waived."

Five working days to review the medical record before the hearing — the notice form: a resident who wishes to review it must be allowed to see it "no later than five working days prior to the" "hearing."

Sixty days of therapeutic leave per calendar year — Clinical Coverage Policy 2B-1, subsection 5.7, quoted above in full.

Fifteen consecutive therapeutic leave days without prior approval — subsection 5.7: no more than that many days "may be taken without approval" "of the DMA," which requires "prior approval on behalf of the beneficiary through" "DMA’s fiscal agent for therapeutic leave that exceeds 15 consecutive days."

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the hearing office to determine.

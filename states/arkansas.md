# Arkansas

## Docket

**Grounds stated in the sources.** 20 CAR § 400-2505(a)(1) of the Rules for Nursing Homes, in the Code of Arkansas Rules (site last updated 8/31/2026): "A resident may be transferred or discharged only for:" "Medical reasons;" "His or her welfare or the welfare of other residents;" "The resident presents a danger to the safety or health of other residents;" "Because the resident no longer needs the services provided by the facility;" "Nonpayment for his or her stay; or" "The facility ceases operation." The rule adds that "Medical reasons" for transfer or discharge "Shall be based on the resident's needs; and" "Are to be determined and documented by a physician." (§ 400-2505(a)(3)(B)).

**Notice stated in the sources.** Two periods in the same current Part. § 400-2505(b): "A minimum of thirty (30) days written notice must be given." and (c): "Transfer for the welfare of the resident or other residents may be affected immediately if such action is documented in the medical record." § 400-411(7)(B), in the Part's admission, transfer and discharge policies: "The resident or responsible party will be provided written notification of his or her transfer ten (10) days prior to the transfer." Both are quoted as published. The notice's required content, § 400-2505(d)(2)(A): "The written notice of transfer or discharge shall state the reason for the proposed transfer or discharge." and "The notice shall inform the resident that they have the right to appeal the decision to the Secretary of the Department of Human Services within seven (7) calendar days." "Reasonable notice of transfer or discharge" means the decision "Shall be discussed with the resident; and" "The resident will be told the reason or reasons and alternatives available." (§ 400-2505(a)(3)(C)).

**Hearing office in these sources.** The rule routes the appeal to the Secretary of the Department of Human Services: "An appeals process for residents objecting to transfer or discharge shall be developed by the facility, in accordance with Arkansas Code § 20-10-1005, as amended." (§ 400-2505(d)(1)); the notice must state the seven-calendar-day right to appeal to the Secretary; "The resident must be assisted by the facility in filing the written objection to transfer or discharge;" "Within fourteen (14) days of the filing of the written objections a hearing will be scheduled; and" "A final determination in the matter will be rendered within seven (7) days of the hearing." The rule names no office, address or form. The department's File an Appeal page, which does not mention nursing facility discharges, prints, for the department's administrative hearings generally, "You may email your request to DHS.Appeals@dhs.arkansas.gov or" "You may send your request by mail to:" "Department of Human Services" "Office of Appeals and Hearings" "P.O. **Ombudsman in these sources.** The program's own site, arombudsman.dhs.arkansas.gov, names "Charlotte Sudmeyer" "State Ombudsman" and "Mandy Mercer" "Ombudsman Coordinator", lists regional ombudsmen by region, and offers a county search ("You can search for your local LTC Ombudsman in the table below:"). ## Lede

This page assembles what Arkansas's own publishers state about involuntary transfer and discharge from a nursing home. The fullest single source is 20 CAR § 400-2505, Transfer or discharge, in Subpart 25 (Residents' Rights) of Part 400, the Rules for Nursing Homes, as the Secretary of State publishes it in the Code of Arkansas Rules, which states six grounds, a thirty-day written notice with an immediate-transfer exception, the notice's contents, and a seven-calendar-day appeal to the Secretary of the Department of Human Services with a hearing scheduled within fourteen days. Arkansas's own vocabulary is *transfer or discharge* of a *resident*; the rule defines "transfer" as movement from one facility to another and calls the appeal a *written objection*.

Two publishers hold the pieces, and one of them was hard to reach. The Secretary of State publishes the Office of Long-Term Care's rules; the Department of Human Services publishes its appeals office and its ombudsman program, on a main site that refuses automated fetches and a program site that prints no contact in its text. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

## 01 — The grounds, as Arkansas describes them

20 CAR § 400-2505(a), Transfer or discharge (Rules for Nursing Homes, Subpart 25, Residents' Rights) — [Arkansas Secretary of State, Code of Arkansas Rules](https://codeofarrules.arkansas.gov/Rules/Rule?levelType=section&titleID=20&chapterID=38&subChapterID=50&partID=1233&subPartID=7678&sectionID=50213), site last updated 8/31/2026, no per-section date, retrieved 2026-09-04 — states that "A resident may be transferred or discharged only for:", in the rule's own order:

"Medical reasons;"

"His or her welfare or the welfare of other residents;"

"The resident presents a danger to the safety or health of other residents;"

"Because the resident no longer needs the services provided by the facility;"

"Nonpayment for his or her stay; or"

"The facility ceases operation."

The rule adds two qualifications reaching the same grounds. "The term "transfer" applies to the movement of the resident from facility to another facility." (§ 400-2505(a)(3)(A)). And "Medical reasons" for transfer or discharge "Shall be based on the resident's needs; and" "Are to be determined and documented by a physician." (§ 400-2505(a)(3)(B)). The rule's own heading for this section reads "6 CAR § 400-2505" where the page's title and citation read "20 CAR § 400-2505"; that is the publisher's text as served, a finding recorded in the change log below and left as found.

## 02 — The published process

### Requesting the hearing

§ 400-2505(d)(1): "An appeals process for residents objecting to transfer or discharge shall be developed by the facility, in accordance with Arkansas Code § 20-10-1005, as amended." (d)(2): "The process shall include:" "The written notice of transfer or discharge shall state the reason for the proposed transfer or discharge." "The notice shall inform the resident that they have the right to appeal the decision to the Secretary of the Department of Human Services within seven (7) calendar days." "The resident must be assisted by the facility in filing the written objection to transfer or discharge;" "Within fourteen (14) days of the filing of the written objections a hearing will be scheduled; and" "A final determination in the matter will be rendered within seven (7) days of the hearing."

No pendency statement is in the sources reviewed: § 400-2505, § 400-2507 (Exercise of rights), and § 400-411 (Admission, transfer, and discharge policies) do not say whether a resident remains at the facility while a written objection is pending. Not stated in the sources reviewed.

The rule names no office, address, or filing form for the Secretary of the Department of Human Services. The department's own File an Appeal page — captured by browser because humanservices.arkansas.gov returns a bare nginx 403 to curl on every path and header set tried — publishes, for the department's administrative hearings generally: "You may email your request to DHS.Appeals@dhs.arkansas.gov or" "You may send your request by mail to:" "Department of Human Services" "Office of Appeals and Hearings" "P.O. Box 1437, Slot S101" "Little Rock, Arkansas 72203-1437". That page does not mention nursing facility discharges anywhere in its text, and whether a § 400-2505 objection goes to that office is not stated in the sources reviewed.

### The notice's required contents

§ 400-2505(a)(3)(C): "Reasonable notice of transfer or discharge" means the decision to transfer or discharge a resident "Shall be discussed with the resident; and" "The resident will be told the reason or reasons and alternatives available." The written notice itself, under (d)(2)(A), "shall state the reason for the proposed transfer or discharge" and "shall inform the resident that they have the right to appeal the decision to the Secretary of the Department of Human Services within seven (7) calendar days". Separately, § 400-411(7)(B) states that "The resident or responsible party will be provided written notification of his or her transfer ten (10) days prior to the transfer." No requirement that the notice carry the ombudsman's contact information is stated in the sources reviewed.

### Bed-hold and return

The Department of Human Services' Medical Assistance Program Manual Of Cost Reimbursement Rules For Long Term Care Facilities, Chapter 6 ("(July 1, 1999)" "(Last Revised 08/01/2022)"), states: "Facilities that certify that occupancy levels are eighty-five percent (85%) or greater will be paid up to five (5) consecutive days for a leave of absence (LOA) to the hospital." The same chapter states, for a different kind of leave: "Facilities may bill for up to fourteen consecutive days for therapeutic home visits regardless of the occupancy rate." Its census-coding key marks such days "Paid Hospital Leave, Bed Reserved" and "Paid Therapeutic Home Leave, Bed Reserved". No readmission or return provision accompanies this allowance in the manual, and none is stated in the Office of Long-Term Care's Rules for Nursing Homes (20 CAR Part 400, searched in full for this row). Not stated in the sources reviewed.

*The three rows above hoist the questions a facility's counsel answers in the first fifteen minutes. They are quotations from Arkansas's published sources, not a determination that any of them applies to any situation.*

### Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

Thirty days' written notice — § 400-2505(b): "A minimum of thirty (30) days written notice must be given."

Immediate-transfer exception — § 400-2505(c): "Transfer for the welfare of the resident or other residents may be affected immediately if such action is documented in the medical record."

Ten days' written notification of a transfer — § 400-411(7)(B): "The resident or responsible party will be provided written notification of his or her transfer ten (10) days prior to the transfer." Both this period and the thirty-day period above are stated in the same current Part; neither is reconciled here.

Seven calendar days to appeal — § 400-2505(d)(2)(A): the resident has "the right to appeal the decision to the Secretary of the Department of Human Services within seven (7) calendar days."

Fourteen days to schedule the hearing — § 400-2505(d)(2)(B): "Within fourteen (14) days of the filing of the written objections a hearing will be scheduled; and"

Seven days for a final determination — § 400-2505(d)(2)(C): "A final determination in the matter will be rendered within seven (7) days of the hearing."

Five consecutive days of paid hospital leave — the cost-reimbursement manual, Chapter 6: facilities "will be paid up to five (5) consecutive days for a leave of absence (LOA) to the hospital", conditioned on the occupancy certification the same chapter states.

Fourteen consecutive days of paid therapeutic home leave — the cost-reimbursement manual, Chapter 6: facilities "may bill for up to fourteen consecutive days for therapeutic home visits regardless of the occupancy rate."

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the hearing office or the relevant state process to determine.

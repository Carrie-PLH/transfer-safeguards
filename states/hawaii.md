**Grounds stated in the sources.** Not quotable from the sources reviewed, for a reason about the transport and not about the state. Hawaii states its nursing-facility rules in Chapter 11-94.2 of the Hawaii Administrative Rules, adopted September 16, 2022, which the Department of Health publishes only as a scanned image: the file's text layer covers the cover sheet alone, and the cover is all this page can quote — "Repeal of Chapter 11-94.1," and "Adoption of Chapter 11-94 .2," under the heading "Hawaii Administrative Rules" and the date "September 16, 2022", with a two-item summary stating that the old chapter is repealed and the new one adopted. The department's Title 11 index lists the chapter as "Nursing Facilities", administered by the "Office of Health Care Assurance", dated 09/16/22. The chapter's own statement of when a facility may transfer or discharge a resident sits in the scanned pages, which a reader can open and read at the link in the source map; this page does not reproduce it. The enumerated federal grounds for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities are assembled on [the federal page](../federal.html), not here.

**Notice stated in the sources.** Not quotable from the sources reviewed, for the same reason: the notice provisions of Chapter 11-94.2 are in the scanned pages. No other Hawaii source captured states a notice period or the notice's required contents for a facility-initiated transfer or discharge.

## Lede

This page assembles what Hawaii's own publishers state about involuntary transfer and discharge from a nursing facility, and it is unusual among these pages in that the fullest single source cannot be quoted. It is a baseline page: the docket rows and the source map are built; the grounds section and the published-process section are not yet written, as the change log records. The operative rule is Chapter 11-94.2 of the Hawaii Administrative Rules, Nursing Facilities, adopted by the Department of Health on September 16, 2022 in the same instrument that repealed its predecessor, 11-94.1. The department publishes it as a 66-page scanned image whose only text is the cover sheet, and no second publisher carries the chapter. This project quotes only text it can verify against a capture, and a machine's reading of a picture is not one, so the rule's grounds, notice and appeal provisions are recorded here as present and unquoted rather than as absent. The file is linked in the source map for a reader to open.

Two publishers hold what can be captured. The Department of Health publishes the chapter's index entry and cover, and, through its Executive Office on Aging, the Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program's contact. The Department of Human Services' Med-QUEST Division publishes Hawaii Medicaid's appeals route, which as published concerns eligibility and health-plan actions and does not mention a facility's transfer or discharge decision. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

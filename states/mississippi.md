# Mississippi

## Docket

**Grounds stated in the sources.** The health department's licensure standard, Rule 45.17.2(4), states them as a resident right: the resident "Is transferred or discharged only for medical reasons, or for his welfare or" "that of other residents, or for nonpayment for his stay (except as prohibited by" "sources of third-party payment)". The Division of Medicaid's rule states no grounds; it states instead four circumstances in which the thirty-day notice may be shortened. Neither captured source enumerates grounds the way the federal regulation does.

**Notice stated in the sources.** Mississippi's two publishers state two different periods. The licensure standard requires that the resident "is given a two week advance notice in" "writing to ensure orderly transfer or discharge. A copy of this notice is" "maintained in his medical record". The Division of Medicaid's Rule 2.20 requires that "The notice of transfer or discharge must be given at least thirty (30) calendar days prior to" "the transfer or discharge unless:" the safety or health of individuals in the facility would be endangered, "The resident no longer requires the level of care provided by the nursing facility,", an immediate move "is required by the resident's urgent medical" "needs, or" "The resident has not resided in the nursing facility for thirty (30) calendar days." Both are quoted as published. On contents, only the Medicaid rule speaks: the notice "must be written, easily understood and include the following information:" the reason, "The effective date of the transfer or discharge,", the location, "A statement that the resident has the right to appeal the action to the appropriate state" "authorities,", "The name, address and telephone number of the State long-term care ombudsman,", and, for residents with developmental disabilities or mental illness, the mailing address and telephone number of the responsible protection and advocacy agency.

**Hearing office in these sources.** Rule 2.20 requires the discharge notice to state "A statement that the resident has the right to appeal the action to the appropriate state" "authorities," without naming one. Title 23 Part 300 (Appeals), the Division of Medicaid's general fair-hearing rule, names the category directly: "Beneficiaries who are subject to a proposed transfer or discharge from a long-term care" "facility or nursing facility," are one of six groups with "appeal processes through the Division of Medicaid" (Rule 2.3.A.4), routed through a four-level process — "Grievance," "Local Hearing," "State Hearing, also referred to as Fair Hearing, and" "Judicial Review." (Rule 2.3.B) — filed in writing, orally, or in person "at a regional office" (Rule 2.7.A), and, where a state hearing is required or requested, "forwarded to the Office of Administrative Appeals" (Rule 2.7.B.3), within "thirty (30) days from the postmark date of the appropriate notice" (Rule 2.8.A). Neither rule names the other, and Part 300 states no street address, phone number or filing form for the regional office, the central office or the Office of Administrative Appeals.

**Ombudsman in these sources.** The State Long-Term Care Ombudsman, in the Department of Human Services, which lists "Discharge and eviction" among what the ombudsman can help with and states that residents have the right to "Appeal a discharge or transfer." Its published contact: "Call the Mississippi statewide Long-Term Ombudsman Help Line: 1-888-844-0041." The programme also states the notice rule in its own words — "The notice must be given at least 30 days before the planned discharge date." — and that the facility "must give a written notice to you, your representative and the Ombudsman program."

## Lede

This page assembles what Mississippi's own publishers state about transfer and discharge from a nursing facility. Three publishers speak here and none of them speaks completely. The health department's licensure standards state the grounds and a two-week notice; the Division of Medicaid's Rule 2.20 states a thirty-day notice, its exceptions and the notice's seven required contents; the ombudsman programme states the thirty-day rule in plain words and points onward to Medicaid for the hearing. Mississippi's vocabulary is *transfer or discharge*, and the Medicaid rule's own heading is *facility initiated discharges*.

Two things a reader should see at once. The two periods do not match — two weeks in the licensing rule, thirty calendar days in the Medicaid rule — and nothing in either document reconciles them. And the appeal route sits in a second Medicaid Part that Rule 2.20 never cites by name: the notice requirement points to "the appropriate state" "authorities," and it is Title 23 Part 300's own Rule 2.3.A.4 — naming beneficiaries facing a nursing-facility transfer or discharge as one of its six appeal categories — that supplies the route this page uses, not a cross-reference either rule states. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

## 01 — The grounds, as Mississippi describes them

Mississippi's two captured publishers do not state grounds the way the federal regulation enumerates them, and neither states a list addressed to the facility as a rule of general application.

The health department's licensure standard, Rule 45.17.2, Residents' Rights (Chapter 45, Minimum Standards for Institutions for the Aged or Infirm), states it as the fourth of the residents' rights the facility's policies must ensure: the resident "Is transferred or discharged only for medical reasons, or for his welfare or that of other residents, or for nonpayment for his stay (except as prohibited by sources of third-party payment)" (Rule 45.17.2(4)).

The Division of Medicaid's Rule 2.20, Facility Initiated Discharges (Title 23 Part 207, Institutional Long Term Care Services, effective 8.1.26), states no counterpart list. What it states instead are the four circumstances under which the standard thirty-day notice period may be shortened, in the rule's own order: "The safety or health of the individuals in the nursing facility would be endangered," "The resident no longer requires the level of care provided by the nursing facility," "An immediate transfer or discharge is required by the resident's urgent medical needs, or" and "The resident has not resided in the nursing facility for thirty (30) calendar days." (Rule 2.20.A.1.a-d). These are drafted as exceptions to a notice period, not as an independent statement of permissible grounds for a facility-initiated discharge, and this page does not read them as one.

No captured Mississippi source enumerates permissible grounds the way the federal floor does. That absence is stated here rather than filled from federal regulation or another state's rule; the federal floor is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html).

## 02 — The published process

### Requesting the hearing

Two rules touch this from different directions, and neither cites the other. Rule 2.20 requires the discharge notice itself to state "A statement that the resident has the right to appeal the action to the appropriate state" "authorities," without naming one. Title 23 Part 300 (Appeals), the Division of Medicaid's general fair-hearing rule, names the category directly: "Beneficiaries who are subject to a proposed transfer or discharge from a long-term care" "facility or nursing facility," are one of six groups with "appeal processes through the Division of Medicaid" (Rule 2.3.A.4). This page treats Part 300 as the route Rule 2.20's own line points toward, on the strength of Rule 2.3.A.4 alone; the connection is this page's, not either publisher's own cross-reference.

Part 300 states a four-level process — "Grievance," "Local Hearing," "State Hearing, also referred to as Fair Hearing, and" "Judicial Review." (Rule 2.3.B) — and states that "All hearing requests may initially be written, oral, or made in-person at a regional office." (Rule 2.7.A). A written request "may be mailed to the regional office or central office." and, separately, "If the hearing involves an issue that requires that a state hearing be held or if a state hearing is requested, the request will be forwarded to the Office of Administrative Appeals." (Rule 2.7.B.2-3). No street address, phone number, or filing form for the regional office, the central office, or the Office of Administrative Appeals is stated in Part 300 as captured.

The filing period: "The applicant or beneficiary has thirty (30) days from the postmark date of the appropriate notice to request either a local or state hearing." (Rule 2.8.A), extendable "if the applicant or beneficiary can show good cause, as determined by the Division of Medicaid, for not filing within thirty (30) days." (Rule 2.8.A). Rule 2.6.B states the same period from a different anchor: the beneficiary "must request a hearing within thirty (30) days of the notice of adverse action." (Rule 2.6.B).

Pendency: "When an applicant/beneficiary or representative requests a hearing within the Advance Notice Period, benefits must be continued or reinstated to the benefit level in effect prior to the planned adverse action, if a timely request is filed." (Rule 2.21). A request is timely for this purpose only if "the request must be received by the regional office within ten (10) days from the notice date of the adverse action." (Rule 2.21.A.1) — a shorter window than the thirty days allowed to request a hearing at all. Rule 2.21 is written for Medicaid "benefits" generally and never uses the words "discharge" or "transfer"; whether it reaches continued residence at the nursing facility during a Rule 2.20 appeal is not stated in the sources reviewed.

### The notice's required contents

Rule 2.20.A.2: the notice "must be written, easily understood and include the following information:" "The reason for the transfer or discharge," "The effective date of the transfer or discharge," "The location to which the resident is being transferred or discharged," "A statement that the resident has the right to appeal the action to the appropriate state" "authorities," "The name, address and telephone number of the State long-term care ombudsman," "For residents with developmental disabilities, the mailing address and telephone" "number of the agency responsible for the protection and advocacy of developmentally" "disabled individuals, and" "For residents with mental illness, the mailing address and telephone" "number of the agency responsible for the protection and advocacy of mentally ill individuals." (Rule 2.20.A.2.a-g). The licensure standard, Rule 45.17.2(4), states none of these contents; only the Medicaid rule speaks to what the notice must contain.

### Bed-hold and return

The Division of Medicaid's Rule 2.8, Temporary Leave (Title 23 Part 207), is Mississippi's captured bed-hold provision; the licensure standard states none. Before a leave begins, "the nursing facility must provide a written notice to the resident and/or family member or legal" "representative explaining the nursing facility's temporary leave, bed-hold and resident return" "policies," (Rule 2.8.B) which "must define the period of time during which the resident is permitted" "to return and resume residence in the nursing facility" (Rule 2.8.B.1) and "must also state that if the resident's absence exceeds the Division of" "Medicaid's bed-hold limit the resident will be readmitted to the nursing facility upon the" "first availability of a semi-private bed if the resident still requires the services provided" "by the nursing facility." (Rule 2.8.B.2).

Mississippi Medicaid covers two kinds of temporary leave, on different limits. Home or therapeutic leave: "up to fifteen (15) consecutive days of home/therapeutic" "temporary leave per one (1) absence for up to a total of forty-two (42) days per state fiscal" "year" (Rule 2.8.C). "The nursing facility must reserve the resident's bed in anticipation of the resident's return" "and cannot fill the resident's bed with another resident during the covered period of" "home/therapeutic temporary leave." (Rule 2.8.C.4), and "Nursing facilities cannot refuse to readmit a resident from home/therapeutic temporary" "leave if the facility has billed for home/therapeutic leave days and the resident still" "requires the services provided by the nursing facility." (Rule 2.8.C.5).

Inpatient hospital leave: "fifteen (15) consecutive days of inpatient hospital" "temporary leave per each absence for continuous acute care during an inpatient hospital stay." (Rule 2.8.D), with "no maximum number of inpatient hospital temporary leave days per each state" "fiscal year." (Rule 2.8.D.2). The same bed-reservation duty applies — "The nursing facility must reserve the resident's bed in anticipation of the resident's return" "and cannot fill the resident's bed with another resident during the covered period of" "inpatient hospital temporary leave." (Rule 2.8.D.8) — as does a readmission-refusal bar worded slightly differently than the home-leave version: "Nursing facilities cannot refuse to readmit a resident from inpatient hospital temporary" "leave if the facility has billed for inpatient hospital leave days and still" "requires the services provided by the nursing facility." (Rule 2.8.D.7). A resident who "remained in the inpatient hospital receiving acute care and" "returns to any Medicaid certified nursing facility" "is not required to complete a new Pre-Admission Screening (PAS) form." (Rule 2.8.D.6).

### Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

Two weeks' notice — the licensure standard, Rule 45.17.2(4): the resident "is given a two week advance notice in" "writing to ensure orderly transfer or discharge."

Thirty calendar days' notice — the Medicaid rule, Rule 2.20.A.1: "The notice of transfer or discharge must be given at least thirty (30) calendar days prior to" "the transfer or discharge unless:" the four exceptions quoted in section 01 above. Neither this period nor the licensure standard's two weeks is reconciled with the other in either document.

Thirty days to request a hearing — Rule 2.8.A: "The applicant or beneficiary has thirty (30) days from the postmark date of the appropriate notice to request either a local or state hearing." Extendable for good cause under the same rule.

Ten days for a timely continuation-of-benefits request — Rule 2.21.A.1: "the request must be received by the regional office within ten (10) days from the notice date of the adverse action."

Fifteen days to request a state hearing and keep benefits continuing — Rule 2.21.B.2: "If benefits have been continued pending the local hearing, then benefits will continue" "pending a state hearing decision as long as the request for the state hearing is made within" "fifteen (15) days of the date on the Notice of Local Hearing Decision."

Ninety days for a final decision — Rule 2.9: "The Division of Medicaid must take final administrative action on a hearing, whether state" "and/or local, within ninety (90) days of the date of the initial request for a hearing."

Fifteen consecutive days, up to forty-two days a year — home/therapeutic temporary leave, Rule 2.8.C, quoted in full in section 02 above.

Fifteen consecutive days, no annual cap — inpatient hospital temporary leave, Rule 2.8.D, quoted in full in section 02 above.

Twenty-four hours — the gap the nursing facility must let pass before a new leave period can begin once either fifteen-day allowance is exhausted: "a" "new leave of absence for home/therapeutic temporary leave does not begin until the" "resident has returned to the nursing facility for twenty-four (24) hours or longer." (Rule 2.8.C.6), and, for the inpatient allowance, "a" "new leave of absence for acute hospitalization does not begin until the resident has" "returned to the nursing facility for a period of twenty-four (24) hours or longer." (Rule 2.8.D.5).

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the hearing office or the relevant state process to determine.

## Docket

**Grounds stated in the sources.** Not stated in the sources reviewed. Alaska's nursing-facility licensing rule, 7 AAC 12.250 through 7 AAC 12.290, and the general rights section that applies to it, 7 AAC 12.890, enumerate no permissible bases for a facility-initiated transfer or discharge; the words transfer and discharge do not appear in the nursing-facility article at all. The Medicaid coverage rule states one circumstance in which a discharge notice is owed, without calling it a ground: "When a nursing facility's utilization review committee or the department determines that a recipient does not, or in the future will not, require continued nursing facility placement" (7 AAC 140.545(a)). The enumerated federal grounds for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities are assembled on [the federal page](../federal.html), not here.

**Notice stated in the sources.** The Medicaid coverage rule, for a recipient: "Before a nursing facility transfers a recipient to another nursing facility or hospital, the facility shall, at least 10 days before the transfer, provide a written notice" to six listed parties beginning "to the recipient;" (7 AAC 140.540(b)), and on discharge "the nursing facility shall provide, at least 10 days before the date of discharge, a written notice of proposed discharge" to the same parties (7 AAC 140.545(a)). Neither section states the notice's required contents or any exception to the ten days. The department's general notice rule for its own actions: "The department will give written notice to the recipient not later than 10 days before the date the department intends to take action denying, reducing, suspending, or terminating assistance, unless" one of eight stated circumstances applies (7 AAC 49.060). The licensing rule states no notice period.

## Lede

This page assembles what Alaska's own publishers state about involuntary transfer and discharge from a nursing facility. The fullest single source is the Medicaid coverage rule for nursing facilities, 7 AAC 140.540 through 7 AAC 140.585 in the Alaska Administrative Code as the Legislature publishes it, which states a ten-day written notice for a transfer to another facility or hospital and for a discharge, routes a recipient's hearing to 7 AAC 49, and states when the department pays to reserve a bed. Alaska's own vocabulary is *transfer* and *discharge* of a *recipient*: the rule that states a notice period is a Medicaid payment rule and speaks of recipients, not of residents generally, and the licensing rule for nursing facilities states no grounds, no notice period and no appeal route of its own.

Three publishers hold the pieces, and the shape is thin by the state's own choice of where to legislate. The Legislature publishes the Administrative Code, including the Department of Health's licensing chapter, Medicaid coverage chapter and hearing chapter. The Department of Health publishes the hearing-rights notice that gives a fair hearing request its address. The Office of Long Term Care Ombudsman, which sits in the Department of Revenue under the Alaska Mental Health Trust Authority, publishes its own contact. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

## 01 — The grounds, as Alaska describes them

Not stated in the sources reviewed, for a facility-initiated transfer or discharge as such. The nursing-facility licensing article, 7 AAC 12.250 through 7 AAC 12.290 (Alaska Administrative Code, Alaska State Legislature), and the general rights section that applies to it, 7 AAC 12.890, enumerate no permissible bases for a facility to transfer or discharge a resident; the words *transfer* and *discharge* do not occur in the licensing article at all, and 7 AAC 12.890(a)(13) mentions a discharge plan only as a right "to participate in the development of a plan of care, or discharge plan," not as a ground.

What the sources state instead is a trigger for a Medicaid payment rule's own notice, not a list of grounds available to a facility. 7 AAC 140.545(a): "When a nursing facility's utilization review committee or the department determines that a recipient does not, or in the future will not, require continued nursing facility placement, the nursing facility shall provide, at least 10 days before the date of discharge, a written notice of proposed discharge" to the six parties the section lists. This is the only circumstance any source reviewed ties to a discharge, and it is a notice trigger inside a coverage rule, not an enumerated basis in the licensing article's own terms. The enumerated federal grounds for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities are assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here borrows from that page as if Alaska stated it.

## 02 — The published process

### Requesting the hearing

7 AAC 140.540(c): "A recipient who receives notice of a proposed transfer under (b) of this section may request a hearing under 7 AAC 49." 7 AAC 140.545(b) states the identical route for a proposed discharge. 7 AAC 49.030(a): "a request for a hearing within the scope of 7 AAC 49.020 must be made to the department in writing by a recipient, or by a legal representative acting on the recipient's behalf, not later than 30 days after the date of the notice required under 7 AAC 49.060. A hearing request may be accepted after the time limit under this section only if the administrative law judge finds, based on the evidence submitted, that the request for a hearing could not be filed within the time limit." Subsection (b): "Unless the request is denied under 7 AAC 49.080, the department will refer the matter to the office of administrative hearings (AS 44.64.010) as provided under AS 44.64.060 and 2 AAC 64.120."

The filing route itself is published on the department's own hearing-rights form, Uni-08 (Revised 2/1/2025): "You may submit your fair hearing request via:" a mailing address, "Attn: Fair Hearings" "P.O. Box 240808" "Anchorage, AK 99524"; a fax, "(907) 644-8126"; an email, "fairhearings@gainwelltechnologies.com"; or a telephone, "(907) 644-6800 or" "(800) 780-9972". The same form states: "You must submit your request within 30 days of the date on the enclosed letter under the authority of 7 AAC 49.030." It also states: "Per 42 CFR § 431.224 an expedited hearing may be requested if the time otherwise permitted for a hearing would jeopardize your life, health, or ability to attain, maintain, or regain maximum function."

The pendency provision, 7 AAC 49.190: a recipient "will, upon filing that hearing request, have the assistance reinstated retroactively or continued until the date that the final decision is issued, unless the recipient informs the department that the recipient does not want to receive continuing assistance." The Uni-08 form states the same continuation right in the department's own words: "If you are currently receiving benefits and you request a hearing, your benefits may be automatically continued while you wait for the hearing decision." It warns: "If you continue to receive benefits and the hearing authority determines the Department was correct to stop or reduce your benefits, you may be required to repay the cost of those services under the authority of 42 C.F.R. 431.230 (b), 7 AAC 49.190, and 7 AAC 49.200." 7 AAC 49.080: "Not later than 10 days after the department receives a hearing request, the department will either" deny it in writing with stated reasons or "grant the request and immediately refer the request for hearing to the office of administrative hearings for scheduling of a hearing or other appropriate proceeding." 7 AAC 49.180 states the outer limit on a decision: the administrative law judge's proposed decision must allow the commissioner "to issue a final decision as described in 7 AAC 150(c) not later than 90 days after the date on which the department received the request for a hearing."

### The notice's required contents

7 AAC 140.540 and 7 AAC 140.545 state who must receive the transfer or discharge notice — the recipient, the recipient's family or guardian if applicable, the attending physician, and two divisions of the department — and the ten-day timing, but neither section states what the notice itself must contain. That absence is recorded here, not filled from the department's own general notice rule.

The general rule the department publishes for its own proposed actions, 7 AAC 49.070, is a different notice — from the department, of the department's own action to "deny, reduce, suspend, or terminate assistance" — and states what it must contain: "the department will state in the written notice the reasons for the proposed action, including the statute, regulation, or policy upon which that action is based." It further requires the department to inform the recipient "(1) of the recipient's right to a hearing; (2) how to request a hearing; (3) that if requested the department will assist the recipient in making a hearing request; (4) by whom the recipient may be represented or assisted; and (5) the conditions, if any, under which assistance may continue if a hearing is requested." 7 AAC 49.060 states when that department notice is due: "The department will give written notice to the recipient not later than 10 days before the date the department intends to take action denying, reducing, suspending, or terminating assistance, unless" one of eight stated circumstances applies, including where "a change in the level of medical care is prescribed by the recipient's physician" (subsection (6)).

### Bed-hold and return

7 AAC 140.585(a): "Except as provided in (b) of this section, the department will pay for reserving a bed during a planned temporary absence of a recipient from a nursing facility if the absence is not more than 12 consecutive days and the time of departure and return of the recipient are recorded in the nursing facility's records." The section lists what counts: "a visit with relatives and friends" and "leave to participate in therapeutic or rehabilitative programs," including trial visits to alternative care settings, gradually increasing visit lengths to prepare for a return home, and an extended absence for workshop evaluation. Subsection (b): "The department will not pay for reserving a bed during a temporary absence of more than 12 consecutive days, unless the department gives prior authorization for the absence. The department will not pay for reserving a bed if the recipient has total absences in excess of 12 days during a 12-month period, unless the department gives prior authorization for the absence." Subsection (d) states the hospitalization rule separately and less favorably: "If a recipient leaves a nursing facility for hospitalization, the department will not pay to reserve the recipient's bed at the nursing facility."

No first-party statement of a resident's right to return to or resume residence in the facility after a bed-hold period — as distinct from whether Medicaid pays to reserve the bed — was found among the sources reviewed: the licensing article, the rights section, and this Medicaid coverage rule. That absence is recorded here rather than assumed from the payment rule.

*The three rows above hoist the questions a facility's counsel answers in the first fifteen minutes and a resident's family almost never finds alone. They are quotations from Alaska's published sources, not a determination that any of them applies to any situation.*

### Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

Ten days' notice before a transfer to another facility or hospital, and before a discharge — 7 AAC 140.540(b) and 7 AAC 140.545(a), each quoted in full in the docket and above.

Thirty days to request a hearing — 7 AAC 49.030(a), quoted in full above; the same 30 days is what the Uni-08 form states to the recipient directly.

Ten days for the department to act on a hearing request — 7 AAC 49.080, quoted in full above.

Ninety days from the hearing request to a final decision — 7 AAC 49.180, quoted in full above.

Ten days' notice before the department's own proposed action denying, reducing, suspending, or terminating assistance, unless a stated exception applies — 7 AAC 49.060, quoted in full above.

Twelve consecutive days, or twelve days total in twelve months, of Medicaid-reserved bed absence — 7 AAC 140.585(a)–(b), quoted in full above; no reservation is paid for a hospitalization absence at all, per subsection (d).

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the office of administrative hearings or the relevant Alaska process to determine.

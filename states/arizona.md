# Arizona

## Docket

**Grounds stated in the sources.** R9-10-408(A)(1) of the Arizona Administrative Code (Supp. 26-1, March 31, 2026): "A resident is transferred or discharged if:" "The nursing care institution is not authorized or not able to meet the needs of the resident, or" "The resident’s behavior is a threat to the health or safety of the resident or other individuals" at the institution. R9-10-408(B) adds nonpayment as a third basis, conditioned on notice: an administrator may transfer or discharge a resident for failure to pay for residency if "The resident or resident’s representative receives a 30-" "day written notice of transfer or discharge, and" "The 30-day written notice includes an explanation of the resident’s right to appeal the transfer or discharge." The rule is the Department of Health Services' licensing rule for nursing care institutions; it is the only enumeration of grounds in the sources reviewed.

**Notice stated in the sources.** R9-10-408(A)(2): documentation of a resident's transfer or discharge includes "The date of the transfer or discharge;" "The reason for the transfer or discharge;" and "A 30-day written notice except:" "In an emergency, or" "If the resident no longer requires nursing care institution services" as determined by a physician or the physician's designee. For nonpayment, R9-10-408(B) requires the 30-day written notice and its appeal explanation quoted above. Before discharge, written follow-up instructions must include "The state long-term care ombudsman’s name, address, and telephone number;" (R9-10-408(D)(1)(b)). The rule states no other required contents for the notice.

**Hearing office in these sources.** The rule requires the nonpayment notice to explain "the resident’s right to appeal the transfer or discharge." and names no office. The office that publishes a route is the Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System Administration, whose Appeal of a Discharge Form states: "The following information is necessary to file an appeal of transfer or discharge from a registered nursing facility. Please attach discharge paperwork from the nursing facility when submitting this form to the Office of the General Counsel." and prints the office as "Office of the General Counsel" "Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System Administration (AHCCCS)" "150 N. 18th Ave., MD 15013" "Phoenix, AZ 85007" "Phone: 602-417-4232" "Email: NFdischarges@azahcccs.gov". AHCCCS's general appeals page carries a dedicated subsection, "How a Nursing Facility Resident Can File an Appeal of a Transfer or Discharge" printing the same office and stating a pendency rule: "The facility may not transfer or discharge the resident while the appeal is pending" "unless the failure to discharge or transfer would endanger the health or safety of the resident or other individuals in the facility." No filing deadline for a discharge appeal is stated in the sources reviewed.

## Lede

This page assembles what Arizona's own publishers state about involuntary transfer and discharge from a nursing care institution. The fullest single source is R9-10-408, Transfer; Discharge, in Article 4 of Title 9, Chapter 10 of the Arizona Administrative Code, published by the Secretary of State in Supp. 26-1 (March 31, 2026) and last amended effective October 1, 2019, which states two grounds and a conditioned third, a 30-day written notice with two exceptions, and the appeal explanation a nonpayment notice must carry. Arizona's own vocabulary is *transfer or discharge* of a *resident* from a *nursing care institution*; AHCCCS, the state Medicaid agency, calls the same act a discharge from a *registered nursing facility*.

Three publishers hold the pieces. The Secretary of State publishes the Department of Health Services' licensing rule. AHCCCS publishes the appeal form and the office that receives it, its Office of the General Counsel. The Department of Economic Security publishes the Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program's page. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

## 01 — The grounds, as Arizona describes them

R9-10-408(A)(1), the Department of Health Services' licensing rule for nursing care institutions: "A resident is transferred or discharged if:" "The nursing care institution is not authorized or not able to meet the needs of the resident, or" "The resident's behavior is a threat to the health or safety of the resident or other individuals" at the nursing care institution.

R9-10-408(B) adds a third basis, conditioned on notice rather than stated freestanding: "An administrator may transfer or discharge a resident for fail-" "ure to pay for residency if:" "The resident or resident's representative receives a 30-" "day written notice of transfer or discharge, and" "The 30-day written notice includes an explanation of the resident's right to appeal the transfer or discharge."

This is the only enumeration of grounds in the Arizona sources reviewed. AHCCCS's own appeal-of-discharge page, captured for section 02 below, separately quotes the federal standard at 42 CFR 483.15(c)(1) when describing who may use its appeal route; that is the federal floor as AHCCCS states it for its own purposes, not a second Arizona enumeration, and it is not folded into this section. Where AHCCCS's citation and R9-10-408 list different grounds, that is recorded as a finding in the change log rather than reconciled here.

## 02 — The published process

### Requesting the hearing

R9-10-408 requires a nonpayment notice to include "an explanation of the resident's right to appeal the transfer or discharge" (R9-10-408(B)(2)) and names no office, form, address, or deadline for that appeal, for a nonpayment case or any other.

The office that publishes a route is AHCCCS's Office of the General Counsel. Its Appeal of a Discharge Form states: "The following information is necessary to file an appeal of transfer or discharge from a registered nursing facility. Please attach discharge paperwork from the nursing facility when submitting this form to the Office of the General Counsel." and prints the office as "Office of the General Counsel" "Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System Administration (AHCCCS)" "150 N. 18th Ave., MD 15013" "Phoenix, AZ 85007" "Phone: 602-417-4232" "Email: NFdischarges@azahcccs.gov".

AHCCCS's general grievance-and-appeals page carries a dedicated subsection, "How a Nursing Facility Resident Can File an Appeal of a Transfer or Discharge" repeating the same office, address, phone, and email, and adding two things R9-10-408 does not state. First, the federal grounds it says a resident's appeal responds to: citing "42 CFR § 483.15(c)(3)" for the notice requirement and "483.15(c)(1)(i)" for the permitted grounds, the page states that "The facility must permit each resident to remain in the facility, and not transfer or discharge the resident from the facility unless -" the transfer or discharge is necessary for the resident's welfare and needs, the resident's health has improved enough that the facility's services are no longer needed, the safety or health of individuals in the facility is endangered, the resident has failed to pay after reasonable and appropriate notice, or the facility ceases to operate. This is AHCCCS's own statement of the federal standard, not a restatement of R9-10-408, and it lists more grounds than R9-10-408 does; the two are not reconciled here (see the change log). Second, and not stated anywhere in R9-10-408, a pendency rule: "The facility may not transfer or discharge the resident while the appeal is pending" "unless the failure to discharge or transfer would endanger the health or safety of the resident or other individuals in the facility. The facility must document the danger that failure to transfer or discharge would pose."

No captured Arizona source states a filing deadline for a nursing-facility transfer-or-discharge appeal specifically. AHCCCS's page states a ten-day continuation-of-services deadline elsewhere, for a health-plan or fee-for-service member's appeal of a reduced, suspended, or terminated service generally; that subsection does not mention discharges, and this page does not apply it to a nursing-facility transfer or discharge appeal. That absence is stated here rather than filled from the general rule.

### The notice's required contents

R9-10-408(A)(2): documentation of a resident's transfer or discharge must include "The date of the transfer or discharge;" "The reason for the transfer or discharge;" and "A 30-day written notice except:" "In an emergency, or" "If the resident no longer requires nursing care institution services" as determined by a physician or the physician's designee. It must also include "A notation by a physician or the physician's desig-" "nee if the transfer or discharge is due to any of the reasons listed in subsection (A)(1)" and, where applicable, "actions taken by a personnel member to protect the resident or other individuals if the resi-" "dent's behavior is a threat to the health and safety of the resident or other individuals in the nursing care institution."

For a nonpayment transfer or discharge, R9-10-408(B) requires the same 30-day written notice, plus "an explanation of the resident's right to appeal the transfer or discharge." quoted above.

Separately, before a discharge (except in an emergency), R9-10-408(D)(1) requires the director of nursing to ensure written follow-up instructions are developed that include "Information necessary to meet the resident's need for medical services and nursing services" and "The state long-term care ombudsman's name, address, and telephone number;". A copy goes to the resident or representative, and a discharge summary is separately developed covering the resident's medical condition, medical and psychosocial history, the date, and the resident's post-discharge location (R9-10-408(D)(2)-(3)).

### Bed-hold and return

R9-10-408, the licensing rule, states no bed-hold provision. Arizona's Medicaid bed-hold rule is R9-28-204(D)(3), in the Arizona Long-Term Care System (ALTCS) chapter of the Arizona Administrative Code: "Bed hold days as authorized by the Administration or its designee for a fee-for-service provider shall meet the fol-" "lowing criteria:"

"Short-term hospitalization leave for a member age 21 and over is limited to 12 days per AHCCCS ben-" "efit year, and is available if a member is admitted to a hospital for a short stay. After the short-term hos-" "pitalization, the member is returned to the institu-" "tional facility from which leave is taken, and to the same bed if the level of care required can be pro-" "vided in that bed" (R9-28-204(D)(3)(a)).

"Therapeutic leave for a member age 21 and older is limited to nine days per AHCCCS benefit year. A physician order is required for therapeutic leave from the facility for one or more overnight stays to enhance psycho-social interaction, or as a trial basis for discharge planning. After the therapeutic leave, the member is returned to the same bed within the institutional facility" (R9-28-204(D)(3)(b)).

"Therapeutic leave and short-term hospitalization leave are limited to any combination of 21 days per benefit year for a member under age 21" (R9-28-204(D)(3)(c)).

### Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

Thirty days, general notice, with two exceptions — R9-10-408(A)(2)(c): documentation must include "A 30-day written notice except:" "In an emergency, or" "If the resident no longer requires nursing care institution services" as a physician or the physician's designee determines.

Thirty days, nonpayment notice — R9-10-408(B)(1): "The resident or resident's representative receives a 30-" "day written notice of transfer or discharge, and"

Pendency during an appeal — AHCCCS's grievance-and-appeals page, quoted in full in section 02 above: "The facility may not transfer or discharge the resident while the appeal is pending" "unless the failure to discharge or transfer would endanger the health or safety of the resident or other individuals in the facility."

Twelve days a year, short-term hospitalization bed-hold — R9-28-204(D)(3)(a), quoted in full in section 02 above.

Nine days a year, therapeutic-leave bed-hold — R9-28-204(D)(3)(b), quoted in full in section 02 above.

Twenty-one days a year, combined, for a member under 21 — R9-28-204(D)(3)(c), quoted in full in section 02 above.

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the hearing office or the relevant state process to determine.

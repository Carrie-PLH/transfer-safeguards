# Oregon

## Docket

**Grounds stated in the sources.** OAR 411-088-0020 groups them. Under "MEDICAL AND WELFARE REASONS." a resident may be moved when the physician states in writing that "The resident's health has improved sufficiently so the resident no longer needs the services provided by the facility; or" that "The facility is unable to meet the resident's needs and the facility has identified another environment available to the resident that may better meet the resident's needs." — and on that ground "The Department shall assist the facility in the facility's effort to identify another environment for the resident." Others follow: a written statement by "the Department Administrator or the State Fire Marshal" that safety is endangered; behaviour creating "a serious and immediate threat" where "all reasonable alternatives to transfer (consistent with the attending physician's orders) have been attempted and documented in the resident's medical record"; a medical emergency; governmental action revoking or not renewing the license; termination of operations; and the end of post-hospital extended care services. "NON-PAYMENT REASONS." are separate, and limited: "A resident may not be transferred if, prior to actual transfer, delinquent charges are paid." and not at all if payment is available through Medicaid, Medicare or another third party. A further basis is stated for "CONVICTION OF A SEX CRIME."

**Notice stated in the sources.** OAR 411-088-0070 sets five periods by ground. Ordinarily, "A facility must provide a resident transferred from the facility a minimum of 30 days prior written notice unless otherwise provided under this section." For a life or safety threat or a behaviour problem amounting to an emergency, fewer than thirty days, but "the facility must give as much prior written notice as the emergency permits." For a medical emergency, none in advance — though "the facility must give written notice before giving the resident's bed to another person." For governmental action, "a minimum of 14 days prior written notice." For a voluntary closure, "written notice must be provided 60 days prior to facility closure." Where the right of return or readmission is denied, the facility must notify the resident immediately and send written notice "within five days from the date of request for return or readmission." The rule requires no inline list of contents; instead it requires the notice to be issued on one of three standard department forms, which state the contents themselves — see section 02.

**Hearing office in these sources.** The Department, in two stages, and the move is frozen while they run. Under OAR 411-088-0080, a resident to be involuntarily transferred or refused return "is entitled to an informal conference and hearing as provided in this rule." The request goes "on the form provided on the brochure, "Leaving the Nursing Facility"." and "must be mailed to the Department within 10 business days of the service or delivery of the notice", with an extension available for good cause. The Department's own standard notice form, Form 0509, states that same period differently — "within 10 calendar days" — and supplies the address the rule itself does not: "ODHS Safety Oversight & Quality" "Nursing Facility Licensing Unit" "P.O. Box 14530" "Salem, OR 97309", email "NF.Licensing@odhs.oregon.gov", fax "503-378-8966". Both periods are quoted as published; this page does not decide whether business days or calendar days governs. The stay is broad: "A resident may not be transferred after having requested an informal conference or after facility staff or the licensee has knowledge of any indication of a resident's desire for an informal conference" until the request is disposed of to everyone's satisfaction or a hearings officer authorises the move. The conference happens "in no event later than 10 days after the request is received", at the facility unless both sides agree otherwise. If the licensee still wants to proceed, the Department asks whether the resident wants a hearing; the hearing is a contested case under ORS chapter 183, held "no later than 30 days after the informal conference", and the hearings officer issues the final order.

**Ombudsman in these sources.** The Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, an agency of its own, at "830 D St. NE, Salem, OR 97301", with "(800) 522-2602", "(503) 378-6533", fax "(503) 373-0852" and "ltco.info@rights.oregon.gov". The same office houses a Residential Facilities Ombudsman and the Oregon Public Guardian.

## Lede

This page assembles what Oregon's own publishers state about involuntary transfer from a nursing facility. Almost all of it lives in one division of the administrative rules, OAR chapter 411 division 88, which Oregon calls its Transfer Rules and which states the grounds, five notice periods, the notification list, the right of return and readmission, the informal conference, and the hearing. A second first-party document, the department's own standard notice form (Form 0509, "Notice of Involuntary Transfer"), is the document the rule requires a facility to issue and states, in the department's own words, the hearing rights a resident actually receives — including a street address the rule itself never states. Oregon's vocabulary is *involuntary transfer*: the rules treat discharge as a kind of transfer rather than as a separate event.

Two things make Oregon distinctive among the states built so far. The first is the informal conference — a state-run meeting held at the facility within ten days, whose purpose is stated as resolving the matter without a formal hearing, and which sits ahead of the contested-case hearing rather than replacing it. The second is how early the move is frozen: not on filing an appeal, but as soon as the facility has "knowledge of any indication of a resident's desire for an informal conference". The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

## 01 — The grounds, as Oregon describes them

OAR 411-088-0010: "Unless a transfer is voluntary, no resident may be transferred from a facility except for the reasons and according to the procedures described in these Transfer Rules."

OAR 411-088-0020 groups the reasons under three headings, in the rule's own order.

"MEDICAL AND WELFARE REASONS." A resident may be transferred when the resident's physician states in writing that "The resident's health has improved sufficiently so the resident no longer needs the services provided by the facility; or" that "The facility is unable to meet the resident's needs and the facility has identified another environment available to the resident that may better meet the resident's needs." On that second basis, "The Department shall assist the facility in the facility's effort to identify another environment for the resident." A resident may also be transferred when "the Department Administrator or the State Fire Marshal states in writing the safety of the resident (or other people in the facility) is endangered and justifies the transfer"; when "the behavior of the resident creates a serious and immediate threat to the resident or to other residents or people in the facility and all reasonable alternatives to transfer (consistent with the attending physician's orders) have been attempted and documented in the resident's medical record"; "when the resident has a medical emergency"; "when governmental action results in a facility's certification or license being revoked or not renewed"; when the facility "intends to terminate operation as a nursing facility"; and when a resident receiving post-hospital extended care or specialized services "has improved sufficiently and no longer needs" those services, according to the physician's written opinion.

"NON-PAYMENT REASONS." A resident may be transferred "when there is a non-payment of facility charges for the resident and payment for the stay is not available through Medicaid, Medicare, or other third party reimbursement." The ground is narrowed: "A resident may not be transferred if, prior to actual transfer, delinquent charges are paid."

"CONVICTION OF A SEX CRIME." A further, separately stated basis applies where the facility learns the resident is required to register as a sex offender or is on supervision after such a conviction and "presents a current risk of harm to another resident, staff, or visitor in the facility as evidenced by:"

## 02 — The published process

### Requesting the hearing

OAR 411-088-0080(1): a resident to be involuntarily transferred or refused the right of return or readmission "is entitled to an informal conference and hearing as provided in this rule." The request is made "on the form provided on the brochure, "Leaving the Nursing Facility"." and "must be mailed to the Department within 10 business days of the service or delivery of the notice. The Department shall immediately notify the licensee of the request." The Department may extend that period "if the Department determines that good cause exists for failure to make a timely request." The Department's own standard notice form, Form 0509, states the same filing step in different units and with an address the rule does not carry: "you must notify the Nursing Facility Licensing Unit within 10 calendar days from the date you receive this notice at: NF.Licensing@odhs.oregon.gov." Its own filing-instructions panel adds a mailing address and a fax number: "ODHS Safety Oversight & Quality" "Nursing Facility Licensing Unit" "P.O. Box 14530" "Salem, OR 97309", "Fax: 503-378-8966", "Telephone: 503-373-2227", and instructs the reader to "ask to speak to the Nursing Facility Policy Analyst." Both the "10 business days" and "10 calendar days" statements are quoted as published; this page does not decide which governs.

The pendency provision is broad and triggers early: "A resident may not be transferred after having requested an informal conference or after facility staff or the licensee has knowledge of any indication of a resident's desire for an informal conference until: (A) Disposition of the request has been completed to the satisfaction of all parties; or (B) Authorization for transfer is provided by a Hearings Officer pursuant to this rule." Any staff member who receives "any oral or written indication of a desire for an informal conference" must immediately notify the administrator, who must immediately notify the Department. The Department "shall hold an informal conference as promptly as reasonably possible but in no event later than 10 days after the request is received" unless a later date is agreed upon, held "at the facility where the resident is located unless an alternate site is agreed upon by both the licensee and the person or agency requesting the informal conference." If a resolution is reached, "the resolution shall be reduced to writing and no formal hearing shall be held." If the licensee still wants to proceed, the Department asks whether a hearing is wanted; a hearing "is conducted as a contested case in accordance with the Administrative Procedures Act, ORS Chapter 183, and the rules of the Department adopted there under" and "must be held no later than 30 days after the informal conference" unless a later date is agreed. Form 0509 adds that "Hearings are held by the Office of Administrative Hearings, which is independent from the Oregon Department of Human Services (ODHS)." and that a party dissatisfied with the Final Order may "ask the Administrative Law Judge to rehear the hearing or reconsider this Order" (ORS 183.480-183.497).

### The notice's required contents

OAR 411-088-0070 does not state the notice's contents as an inline list. Instead, section (4): "STANDARD NOTICE REQUIRED. Written notice must be provided using Form 0509 (Notice of Transfer), Form 0510 (Denial of Readmission/Return), or Form 0509L (Resident Letter Nursing Facility Closure), as appropriate." The notice to the resident "must be accompanied by a copy of the Aging and People with" — the source's own page break falls inside this sentence — "Disabilities Division's brochure, "Leaving the Nursing Facility" (Form 9847)." That brochure is still capture-pending, as at baseline. Form 0509 itself is the source for what the standard form actually contains: fields for the date and location of the proposed transfer, the issuing facility, and the resident; a checklist of the specific OAR 411-088-0020 ground or grounds relied on; a space for the facility to explain its reason in more detail; and a "Your Hearing Rights" section stating the resident's filing route (quoted above), the Long-Term Care Ombudsman's contact, and, for "Nursing Facility residents with:" "Intellectual and developmental disabilities or related disabilities" or "Mental disorder or related disabilities established under the
       Protection and Advocacy for Mentally Ill Individuals Act.", the contact for Disability Rights Oregon, the state's protection-and-advocacy agency: "Disability Rights Oregon", "610 SW Broadway, Suite 200", "Portland, OR 97205", "Voice: 503-243-2081", fax "1-503-243-1738".

On distribution, section (3): notice must go to "The resident or former resident, as appropriate" to everyone listed in the resident's record under section (2), to "The local unit of the Aging and People with Disabilities Division or Type B Area Agency on Aging" (unless the resident is private-pay and has stayed thirty days or less), and to "The Long-Term Care Ombudsman if there is no one currently involved and designated by the resident." A closure notice must go to the ombudsman regardless.

### Bed-hold and return

OAR 411-088-0050, "Right to Return from Hospital": "If a resident is transferred to a hospital, the facility may not fill the resident's bed with another person if the resident or the resident's legal representative offers payment, or reimbursement is available from the Department, for the period of the hospital stay." Where payment or reimbursement is offered or available, "the resident shall have the right of return to his or her bed immediately after the period of hospital stay." The facility administrator "is responsible for notifying the resident or legal representative and any agency responsible for the welfare or support of the resident of the option to offer payment to hold the bed prior to filling the bed with another person." That notification is documented by the resident's or representative's written agreement or rejection. Where "the resident's bed has been given to another person because payment was not offered, the resident shall have priority for readmission over all other persons with a right to readmission and over any waiting list." "Persons with right of return have priority over all persons with right of readmission." A facility that denies a right of return "shall give written notice" (Form 0510).

OAR 411-088-0060, "Right to Readmission": "Any person transferred from a facility voluntarily or involuntarily shall have the right of readmission to the facility from which the person was transferred, provided that: (a) A request for readmission is made within 180 days of the date of transfer; (b) The person is eligible by means of payment and requires nursing facility care; and (c) No determination was made at informal conference or hearing that the person does not have the right of readmission." The rule does not require a facility to admit a person to "a bed located in a room which is occupied by a resident of the opposite sex at the time of the request." An exception applies where the two residents previously shared a room and neither objects, and does not require re-acceptance of someone who "voluntarily transferred from the facility directly to another nursing facility." "A former resident who receives Medicaid does not have the right to be readmitted to a facility which is not Medicaid certified unless reimbursement is available pursuant to OAR 411-070-0010" — that cross-referenced rule is not itself captured here. A person "whose stay in the facility totals 30 or fewer days" and was transferred for post-hospital extended care or specialized services "may not have a right of readmission."

### Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

Thirty days, ordinarily — OAR 411-088-0070(1)(a): "A facility must provide a resident transferred from the facility a minimum of 30 days prior written notice unless otherwise provided under this section."

As much notice as the emergency permits, for a life or safety threat or behaviour emergency — section (1)(b): "the facility must give as much prior written notice as the emergency permits."

No advance notice, for a medical emergency — section (1)(c): the facility "must give written notice before giving the resident's bed to another person."

Fourteen days, for governmental action — section (1)(e): "a minimum of 14 days prior written notice."

Sixty days, before a voluntary closure — section (1)(g)(A): "written notice must be provided 60 days prior to facility closure."

Five days, to notify a resident denied return or readmission — section (1)(f): written notice "mailed (registered or certified) or delivered in person within five days from the date of request for return or readmission."

Ten business days, or ten calendar days, to request an informal conference — OAR 411-088-0080(2)(a)(A): "must be mailed to the Department within 10 business days of the service or delivery of the notice"; Form 0509: "you must notify the Nursing Facility Licensing Unit within 10 calendar days from the date you receive this notice". Both are quoted; this page does not decide which governs.

Ten days, for the Department to hold the informal conference — OAR 411-088-0080(3)(a): "in no event later than 10 days after the request is received".

Thirty days, for the hearing after the informal conference — OAR 411-088-0080(4)(b): "the hearing must be held no later than 30 days after the informal conference."

One hundred eighty days, to request readmission — OAR 411-088-0060(1)(a): "A request for readmission is made within 180 days of the date of transfer".

Thirty days or fewer, a stay length that forfeits the readmission right — OAR 411-088-0060(7): a person "whose stay in the facility totals 30 or fewer days" transferred under the post-hospital extended-care ground "may not have a right of readmission."

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the hearing office to determine.

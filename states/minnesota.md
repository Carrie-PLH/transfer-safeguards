# Minnesota

## Docket

**Grounds stated in the sources.** No enumeration of permissible bases appears in Minnesota's captured statutes or in Minnesota Rules chapter 4658, the state's nursing-home rule chapter, which was reviewed in full and states none. What § 144.651, subdivision 29 states is a standard rather than a list: "Residents shall not be arbitrarily transferred or discharged." The same subdivision names circumstances that may shorten the notice period — "a determination by utilization review, the accommodation of newly admitted residents, a change in the resident's medical or treatment program, the resident's own or another resident's welfare, or nonpayment for stay unless prohibited by the public program or programs paying for the resident's care, as documented in the medical record" — but states them as reasons to shorten notice, not as grounds for the move. The federal enumeration is quoted on [the federal page](../federal.html) and is not restated here.

**Notice stated in the sources.** § 144.651, subdivision 29: "Residents must be notified, in writing, of the proposed discharge or transfer and its justification no later than 30 days before discharge from the facility and seven days before transfer to another room within the facility." On contents: "This notice shall include the resident's right to contest the proposed action, with the address and telephone number of the area nursing home ombudsman pursuant to the Older Americans Act, section 307(a)(12)." The statute adds that a resident, once informed of the right, "may choose to relocate before the notice period ends", and that "The notice period may be shortened in situations outside the facility's control" as stated.

**Hearing office in these sources.** The commissioner of health, with hearings conducted under the contested-case rules. § 144A.135(b): "To appeal the discharge or transfer, or notification of an intended discharge or transfer, a resident or the resident's representative must request a hearing in writing no later than 30 days after receiving written notice, which conforms to state and federal law, of the intended discharge or transfer." The Department of Health's own "Nursing Home Discharge Appeals" page states the filing route this statute leaves unstated: "To appeal, please complete and submit an HRD Appeals Submission Form", choosing "Nursing Home Discharge Appeal" from the form's "Type of Appeal" dropdown. On timing and place: "Hearings under this section shall be held no later than 14 days after receipt of the request for hearing, unless impractical to do so or unless the parties agree otherwise." and "Hearings shall be held in the facility in which the resident resides, unless impractical to do so or unless the parties agree otherwise." On pendency and remedy: "A resident who timely appeals a notice of discharge or transfer, and who resides in a certified nursing home or boarding care home, may not be discharged or transferred by the nursing home or boarding care home until resolution of the appeal." and "The commissioner can order the facility to readmit the resident if the discharge or transfer was in violation of state or federal law."

**Ombudsman in these sources.** The Office of Ombudsman for Long-Term Care, "an independent state agency that serves people needing or receiving long-term care through complaint investigation, advocacy, and education." Its published contact: "The Office of Ombudsman for Long-Term Care 540 Cedar Street St. Paul, MN 55101 1-800-657-3591". The appeals statute names the office expressly: "Nothing in this section limits the right of a resident or the resident's representative to request or receive assistance from the Office of Ombudsman for Long-Term Care or the Office of Health Facility Complaints with respect to an intended discharge or transfer."

## Lede

This page assembles what Minnesota's own publishers state about transfer and discharge from a nursing home or boarding care home. Three statutes and a Medicaid rule carry most of it: § 144.651, subdivision 29, the health care bill of rights provision that states the notice; § 144A.135, which states the appeal; and Minnesota Rules 9505.0415, the Medicaid rule on reserved beds and leave days. Minnesota's vocabulary is *transfer or discharge*, with a separate and much shorter clock for a move to another room in the same building; the appeal is a *contested case* hearing before the commissioner of health, and the Medicaid rule's own vocabulary is *leave days* and *reserved bed*.

Minnesota's hearing has a feature no other state in this index states: it is held in the facility where the resident lives, unless that is impractical or the parties agree otherwise, and within fourteen days of the request. The federal floor for Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities is assembled separately on [the federal page](../federal.html); nothing here fills a gap from federal law or another state. Confirm applicability with the agencies named in the sources: whether any provision applies to a particular facility, resident, or notice is for the hearing office to determine. Deadlines and periods on this page are quoted from the sources, not calculated for any case.

## 01 — The grounds, as Minnesota describes them

Minnesota's captured statutes state a standard rather than an enumeration. § 144.651, subdivision 29 ([Minnesota Revisor of Statutes](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/144.651), retrieved 2026-09-17): "Residents shall not be arbitrarily transferred or discharged."

The same subdivision names circumstances under which the notice period, not the right to transfer or discharge, may be shortened: "a determination by utilization review, the accommodation of newly admitted residents, a change in the resident's medical or treatment program, the resident's own or another resident's welfare, or nonpayment for stay unless prohibited by the public program or programs paying for the resident's care, as documented in the medical record." These are quoted here because they are the closest thing the statute states to a list of reasons a move might happen sooner than the standard notice allows — they are not read as permissible grounds, and nothing from the federal page is borrowed to fill the gap.

Minnesota Rules chapter 4658, the Department of Health's nursing-home rule chapter, was reviewed part by part against the Revisor's own rule-listing page for the chapter (checked, not quoted, since nothing on this page draws from it directly) for a rule that might enumerate grounds the way a sibling state's administrative code does. None does. The chapter's resident-rights group of parts states policies on room assignment, medication administration, and freedom from restraint, not transfer or discharge grounds. The one part titled for the subject, 4658.0465 ([Minnesota Revisor of Statutes](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/4658.0465/), "Published Electronically: October 11, 2007", retrieved 2026-09-17), is a clinical-record documentation rule: "TRANSFER, DISCHARGE, AND DEATH." states that "When a resident is transferred or discharged for any reason other than death, the nursing home must compile a discharge summary that includes the date and time of transfer or discharge, reason for transfer or discharge, transfer or discharge diagnoses, and condition", and that this summary be sent to a receiving facility — but it states no basis on which a transfer or discharge may be made. This is stated as a searched absence, not inferred from silence alone.

## 02 — The published process

### Requesting the hearing

§ 144A.135(a) ([Minnesota Revisor of Statutes](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/144A.135), retrieved 2026-09-17): "The commissioner shall establish a mechanism for hearing appeals on transfers and discharges of residents by nursing homes or boarding care homes licensed by the commissioner." Paragraph (b): "Until federal regulations are adopted under sections 1819(f)(3) and 1919(f)(3) of the Social Security Act that govern appeals of the discharges or transfers of residents from nursing homes and boarding care homes certified for participation in Medicare or medical assistance, the commissioner shall provide hearings under sections 14.57 to 14.62 and the rules adopted by the Office of Administrative Hearings governing contested cases. To appeal the discharge or transfer, or notification of an intended discharge or transfer, a resident or the resident's representative must request a hearing in writing no later than 30 days after receiving written notice, which conforms to state and federal law, of the intended discharge or transfer."

The statute names no office, address, or form for that written request. The Department of Health's own "Nursing Home Discharge Appeals" page ([health.state.mn.us](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/regulation/ohfc/appeals/nhda.html), "Last Updated: 06/25/2026", retrieved 2026-09-17) states the route: "Residents of nursing homes who are being involuntarily transferred or discharged by the facility have the right to appeal the proposed transfer or discharge. To appeal, please complete and submit an HRD Appeals Submission Form", with instructions to "Choose "Nursing Home Discharge Appeal" from the Type of Appeal dropdown" and to "Please attach a copy of the discharge notice issued by the facility, if available." The same page restates the filing window ("The appeal must be submitted within 30 days of receiving the discharge/transfer notice") and describes what follows: "Upon receiving the appeal, MDH will coordinate with the facility and appellant on potential prehearing and/or hearing dates. MDH will then submit a docket request to the Court of Administrative Hearings." That page's own phrase, "Court of Administrative Hearings", is reproduced as published; Minnesota's contested-case hearings are otherwise conducted by the state's Office of Administrative Hearings, and the two sources are not reconciled here.

On timing and place, § 144A.135(c): "Hearings under this section shall be held no later than 14 days after receipt of the request for hearing, unless impractical to do so or unless the parties agree otherwise. Hearings shall be held in the facility in which the resident resides, unless impractical to do so or unless the parties agree otherwise." The Department of Health's page states the same rule in its own words: "The hearing must be held within 14 calendar days at the facility where the appellant lives unless it would be impractical or the parties agree otherwise."

On pendency and remedy, § 144A.135(d): "A resident who timely appeals a notice of discharge or transfer, and who resides in a certified nursing home or boarding care home, may not be discharged or transferred by the nursing home or boarding care home until resolution of the appeal. The commissioner can order the facility to readmit the resident if the discharge or transfer was in violation of state or federal law. If the resident is required to be hospitalized for medical necessity before resolution of the appeal, the facility shall readmit the resident unless the resident's attending physician, advanced practice registered nurse, or physician assistant documents, in writing, why the resident's specific health care needs cannot be met in the facility." The Department of Health's page states its own version of the pendency rule: "While the appeal is pending, the appellant may not be discharged from the facility."

One statutory exception to pendency is stated at § 144A.135(g): "A person required to inform a health care facility of the person's status as a registered predatory offender under section 243.166, subdivision 4b, who knowingly fails to do so shall be deemed to have endangered the safety of individuals in the facility under Code of Federal Regulations, chapter 42, section 483.12. Notwithstanding paragraph (d), any appeal of the notice and discharge shall not constitute a stay of the discharge."

### The notice's required contents

§ 144.651, subdivision 29 states the notice's contents directly: "Residents must be notified, in writing, of the proposed discharge or transfer and its justification no later than 30 days before discharge from the facility and seven days before transfer to another room within the facility. This notice shall include the resident's right to contest the proposed action, with the address and telephone number of the area nursing home ombudsman pursuant to the Older Americans Act, section 307(a)(12)." A neighboring subdivision of the same statute, quoted in the same source, adds a posting requirement rather than a per-notice one: "Notice of the grievance procedure of the facility or program, as well as addresses and telephone numbers for the Office of Health Facility Complaints and the area nursing home ombudsman pursuant to the Older Americans Act, section 307(a)(12) shall be posted in a conspicuous place."

### Bed-hold and return

Minnesota Rules 9505.0415, the rule captioned LONG-TERM CARE FACILITIES; LEAVE DAYS ([Minnesota Revisor of Statutes](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/9505.0415/), "Published Electronically: October 21, 2014", retrieved 2026-09-17), is the state's Medicaid bed-hold rule. It defines the terms and states the reservation duty: ""Reserved bed" means the same bed that a recipient occupied before leaving the facility for hospital leave or therapeutic leave or an appropriately certified bed if the recipient's physical condition upon returning to the facility prohibits access to the bed he or she occupied before the leave." On payment: "A leave day is eligible for payment under medical assistance, subject to the limitations of this part. The leave day must be for hospital leave or therapeutic leave of a recipient who has not been discharged from the long-term care facility. A reserved bed must be held for a recipient on hospital leave or therapeutic leave."

The rule states two separate payment limits rather than one. For hospital leave: "Payment for leave days for hospital leave is limited to 18 consecutive days for each separate and distinct episode of medically necessary hospitalization." For therapeutic leave, the limit runs by the kind of facility service the recipient receives: "recipients receiving skilled nursing facility services as provided in part 9505.0420, subpart 2, 36 leave days per calendar year", the same period for intermediate care facility services under subpart 3, and "recipients receiving intermediate care facility, developmentally disabled services as provided in part 9505.0420, subpart 4, 72 leave days per calendar year", with up to 48 additional days the commissioner may approve for family activities under stated conditions, and, for "recipients residing in a long-term care facility that has a license to provide services for persons with physical disabilities as provided in parts 9570.2000 to 9570.3400, 72 leave days per calendar year."

The rule states nothing about what happens to a resident's placement once a leave-day limit is exceeded — no readmission or first-available-bed statement, unlike the appeal statute's own hospitalization sentence quoted above. That absence is stated rather than filled; the appeal statute's readmission duty when a resident "is required to be hospitalized for medical necessity before resolution of the appeal" is a distinct provision, tied to a pending appeal rather than to the Medicaid leave-day limit, and is not read as answering the rule's silence.

### Notice periods and deadlines, as stated in the sources

All quoted; none calculated.

Thirty days' notice for a discharge from the facility — § 144.651, subd. 29, quoted in full above.

Seven days' notice for a transfer to another room within the facility — § 144.651, subd. 29, same sentence.

Thirty days to request the hearing — § 144A.135(b), quoted in full above.

Fourteen days to the hearing — § 144A.135(c): "Hearings under this section shall be held no later than 14 days after receipt of the request for hearing, unless impractical to do so or unless the parties agree otherwise."

Eighteen consecutive days of hospital leave per hospitalization episode — Minn. R. 9505.0415, subp. 5, quoted in full above.

Thirty-six or seventy-two therapeutic leave days per calendar year, depending on the facility service the recipient receives, with up to 48 additional days for family activities under stated conditions — Minn. R. 9505.0415, subp. 6, quoted in full above.

What any of these periods means for a particular case is for the hearing office to determine.

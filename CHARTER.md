# transfer-safeguards — project charter

Named 2026-08-29: **Room & Recourse**, at roomandrecourse.com (unregistered
as of this date; registration pending). The repository keeps its working
slug (transfer-safeguards), per the sibling pattern. A project of Field
Assembly LLC. Status: draft. Prepared 2026-08-29. Engine: institutional
procedure (Engine 1), fourth application after Gathered Work, Rules &
Record, and Board & Border.

## What this is

A source-linked index of nursing-home involuntary transfer and discharge
procedure, one page per jurisdiction, built for the resident — or the
family member holding the envelope — who has just received a notice that
the facility intends to move them out, and who does not know that the
grounds are enumerated, the notice has required contents, the decision can
be appealed, and an ombudsman exists whose job is this exact moment. Each
page assembles, quotes, dates, and links the published materials: the
grounds a facility may state, the notice a resident is owed, the office
that hears the appeal and how a hearing is requested, what the state
publishes about bed-hold and readmission, and the state long-term-care
ombudsman's published contact. The page determines nothing and advises
nothing. It makes the state's own statements findable at the moment a
resident discovers they need them.

## Doctrine fit

Thesis test: facility administrators and their counsel work these
procedures routinely and issue notices from templates; the resident
encounters the procedure once, in crisis, often from a hospital bed after
the facility declines to readmit. The asymmetry is near-total: the facility
knows the enumerated grounds, the notice clock, and the appeal deadline;
the resident frequently learns the word "ombudsman" from the notice itself,
if the notice complies at all. The product moves the published rules to the
moment of need without substituting a new dependency — no appeal filing, no
case evaluation, no intake.

Selection criteria: individual versus institution where the institution
administers the rules: yes — the facility initiates the action and drafts
the notice, and its procedural fluency is not the resident's. Technically
public but fragmented: yes; the federal floor sits in regulation, the
hearing route in a state administrative-hearings or Medicaid fair-hearing
apparatus, bed-hold policy in the state Medicaid plan materials, and the
ombudsman in a separately administered aging-services program — four
publishers per state before the state's own licensure code is opened.
Short procedural clock: yes, and it is stated in the sources — advance
notice periods, appeal-request deadlines, bed-hold windows. Severe stakes,
low salience: yes; the stake is housing, care continuity, and in the
readmission cases the roof itself, and no one studies this procedure before
receiving the notice. Identifiable moment of need: yes; a dated notice
arrives, or a hospital stay ends and the bed is refused.

The structural advantage carried from the siblings: the unit of assembly is
the jurisdiction, so the index is bounded and finishable. The structural
fact this project shares with Board & Border rather than R&R: a genuine
shared layer with its own publisher. Federal regulation (42 CFR 483.15(c)
on transfer and discharge; 42 CFR part 431 subpart E on fair hearings) sets
a floor for every Medicare/Medicaid-certified facility, and CMS publishes
its own guidance. That layer gets one page of its own, quoting the federal
materials; each state page quotes what that state's own agencies publish.
Neither substitutes for the other, and the state's own shape always wins on
the state's page.

## The reader

The reader is a nursing-home resident or, at least as often, an adult child
or spouse acting for one. Scope starts at Medicare/Medicaid-certified
nursing facilities: assisted living, board-and-care, and other
non-certified settings run on different (state-only) law and are explicitly
out of scope for v1, listed as open questions, because each adds a distinct
source universe per state. The same no-collection posture holds: no
accounts, no intake, no analytics, nothing about any resident or any case
ever touches the work. The reader may be in crisis and reading on a phone
in a hospital corridor; the hoisted rows exist for that reader.

## Hard boundaries (these are the product, verbatim sibling posture)

The page presents potentially relevant official sources. It never
determines whether a particular notice is lawful, never determines whether
particular grounds apply to a situation, never evaluates a facility's
stated reason, never predicts a hearing outcome, never restates deadlines
as advice about when to act, never recommends strategies, and never gives
legal or medical advice. No commentary on facilities, chains, lawsuits,
inspection results, or news. Independence stated in the footer: independent
of every facility and operator, of CMS and every state agency, and of the
ombudsman programs themselves. Absences are findings: where a state
publishes nothing on a question — bed-hold especially, where Medicaid-plan
materials can be genuinely silent or buried — the page says so in the
standing register ("Not stated in the sources reviewed") along with what
was searched. Never fill a gap from federal law or from another state — the
federal floor lives on the federal page, and a state page asserting the
floor without the state's own statement of it is the exact failure this
project exists to avoid. No agency's documents are read more generously or
sceptically than another's.

## Page specification

Adapted from the sibling page structure. Docket, immediately after the
title, five rows on every page:

1. Grounds stated in the sources: the enumerated permissible bases for
   involuntary transfer or discharge as this state's own materials state
   them — quoted, or the absence stated with a pointer to the federal page.
2. Notice stated in the sources: the advance-notice period, the required
   contents, and the stated exceptions, as this state publishes them.
3. Hearing office in these sources: the office that receives the appeal,
   and the filing route (form, address, portal, deadline) exactly as
   published, discrepancies between an agency's own pages shown, not
   reconciled.
4. Ombudsman in these sources: the state long-term-care ombudsman's
   published contact exactly as published — name, address, phone, email.
5. Sources last checked: date matching the index row.

Standing docket note: reproduced from the agencies' own pages and documents
quoted below, potentially relevant official sources, not a determination
that any notice, ground, or deadline applies to any situation.

Lede: what the page assembles, the state's fullest single source named with
its date, the state's own vocabulary introduced (transfer, discharge,
relocation, eviction — states differ in what they call the same act), the
confirm-applicability line, and the standing line that deadlines are quoted
from the sources, not calculated for any case.

01 The grounds, as the state describes them: each permissible basis with a
verbatim quotation and citation, in the state's own order and vocabulary.

02 The published process, opening with three hoisted rows — the questions a
facility's counsel answers in the first fifteen minutes and a resident's
family almost never finds alone:

1. Requesting the hearing: where the appeal goes, by what route, and the
   stated deadline — quoted (the pendency analogue: in many states an
   appeal filed in time means the resident stays put while it is heard, and
   where the state says so, that statement is quoted here).
2. The notice's required contents: what the notice itself must contain as
   the state states it — including, where stated, the appeal-rights text
   and the ombudsman contact the facility must include.
3. Bed-hold and return: what the state publishes about holding the bed
   during a hospital stay and about readmission afterwards — quoted; where
   unaddressed, that absence stated with what was searched.

Standing note after the rows. Then **Notice periods and deadlines, as
stated in the sources**: every stated period quoted with its citation,
opening "All quoted; none calculated," closing with the standing line that
what any of it means for a particular case is for the hearing office to
determine. (This heading is the marker by which the status generator
recognizes a full page — wording kept exact.)

03 Source map: table of documents, locations, double dates; captured and
pending items both listed.

04 Change log: dated entry — what was captured first-party, every finding
numbered, capture-pending list, Internet Archive line, reviewer line
(Carrie Schluter; "review pending before publication" until she states she
has reviewed), corrections line with hello@fieldassembly.net.

Federal layer: one page (site/federal.html, working page federal.md) built
exactly like a state page — its own packet, its own docket, its own change
log — quoting 42 CFR 483.15(c), 42 CFR part 431 subpart E, and CMS's own
published guidance, with double dates. State pages link to it; it never
overrides what a state agency says on the state's page, and where state and
federal materials publish different things, both are quoted and the
discrepancy is a numbered finding — reconciled nowhere.

Standing scope note on the federal page (added 2026-08-31). The federal page
carries one further standing paragraph, immediately after its lede, stating
the condition on which everything the page quotes depends: that these
requirements reach a facility through its participation in Medicare or
Medicaid, that whether a particular facility participates is a fact about
that facility rather than about its state, that this site does not record
it, and that the ombudsman named on the reader's state page can be asked.
The note is a statement of the collection's scope, not of anyone's rights,
and is written to stay one: it says what this site does and does not carry,
names no consequence of participating or not participating, and does not
tell a reader what law governs them. It may quote 42 CFR 483.1(b) for the
publisher's own statement of scope, which is carried in the supplement
tools/packets/federal-packet-scope.txt; everything else in it is Field
Assembly describing its own method and needs no source. This note is the
only place on the site where the relationship between the layers is
described in prose. It does not appear on state pages, whose existing
one-line pointer to the federal page is unchanged, because a scope note
repeated fifty-one times invites the reader to treat it as the answer to
the gap it exists to mark.

## Build order

1. Exemplars first: the federal layer page, then two states bounding the
   format — one where hearing route, bed-hold, and ombudsman sit with
   distinct agencies (the hard shape), one where a single agency publishes
   most of it (the simple shape). Candidates settled at build time from the
   sources, not assumed here.
2. Full-page candidates next by nursing-home resident population, then the
   baseline sprint per QUEUE.md.

## Open questions (v1 scope exclusions)

Assisted living and other non-certified settings. Involuntary moves within
a facility (room changes). Medicaid-eligibility terminations that operate
as constructive removal. Spanish pages (follow the R&R convention when they
begin: published translations only).
